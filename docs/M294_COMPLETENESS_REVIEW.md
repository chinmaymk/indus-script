# M-294 inscription completeness: independent visual review

A complete three-sign inscription is plausible. The surviving photograph and the selected parallels do not establish that no inscription was lost. There is also no positive photographic evidence here that an additional sign was actually lost. The right conclusion is uncertainty about completeness, rather than a restoration of invented signs.

## Sources and identity

Reviewed the [CISI photograph, printed p71](../results/primary_pages/m294-completeness-cisi-photo-crop.png) (PDF107), [CISI metadata, printed p368](../results/primary_pages/m294-cisi-404.png) (PDF404), and [2025 bulletin Figure3, printed p9](../results/primary_pages/m294-completeness-bulletin-15.png) (PDF15). CISI metadata identifies M-294 with Finnish1376, HR4212e, ASI63.10.378 and HU448. Finnish1376 is not asserted to be a Mahadevan text number. [Machine-readable review](../data/processed/m294_completeness_review.json) retains source and derivative SHA256 hashes, page pins, crop coordinates, observations and the limited secondary rows used.

The higher-resolution rendering enlarges the existing scan; it does not create new photographic detail. The crop was made without mirroring, sharpening or reconstruction. The reviewer did not independently inspect the M-1912 original artifact photograph or the photographs of all Figure3c examples.

## What the images show

CISI labels the original seal M-294 A and its mirrored impression M-294 a. The damaged side is photo-right on the original and photo-left on the impression. It is beside the jar-like form, whereas the three short strokes lie toward the other side. Under the bulletin's normalized right-to-left reading, this is the reading-end side. A missing **prefix** is therefore not the direct geometrical inference from this fracture.

The photographs preserve a short stroke group, a central fish-like form and a jar-like form. The three-sign transcription is visually compatible with these forms; the review does not certify every Wells variant distinction. Surviving surface separates the jar-like form from the damaged edge. This makes the author's completeness reconstruction reasonable, but the original outer margin of the clipped side is absent. The scan cannot show how much space originally lay beyond it. No extra partly clipped glyph is securely identifiable in this image.

Bulletin Figure3a is explicitly a representative drawing. Its dashed boundary and restored portions of the animals extend beyond the depicted fracture. These restorations cannot serve as independent measurements of the lost edge or proof that it was blank. The visible animals constrain a plausible reconstruction, not the uniquely correct original width.

## Why the parallels do not prove completeness

A short intact parallel establishes that a short text is possible. It does not imply that every occurrence of the same sequence is a whole inscription. The author's phrase-ending interpretation can support a proposed boundary, but even accepting a phrase boundary would not by itself rule out a further phrase or material on the missing margin. The semantic and grammatical status of that ending is not independently deciphered.

M-1912 is drawn with a pointed ending form; M-294 has a jar-like ending form. The limited secondary rows also distinguish them: M-1912 is `+520-220-003+` and M-294 is `+740-220-003+`. They are a proposed functional analogy, not identical literal strings. Figure3c's M-1439, M-1441, M-1442 and M-1443 are closer literal parallels to M-294. H-669 is another related pattern with a different stroke count. These drawings were visually checked as the author's examples, not independently authenticated as complete artifact photographs.

The parent audit's longer-string search is relevant to the logic of using a local pattern to certify a whole text. Its file-left extensions are the relevant reading-suffix direction for M-294's observed break, conditional on the secondary direction field. Longer inscriptions elsewhere still do not establish that M-294 itself lost any signs. Likewise the secondary export's `complete=Y` is an editorial judgment: the same row says `preservation=fragment` and `condition=Poor`. Its inclusion in a strict corpus subset cannot independently validate completeness.

## Result

Retain the three visible sign groups as evidence, with the exact secondary codes attributed to their source. Treat original inscription completeness as plausible and unresolved. No new semantic or phonetic value follows, and the uncertainty does not erase the independently assessed jewellery-hoard association.
