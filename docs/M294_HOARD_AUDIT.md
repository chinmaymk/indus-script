# M-294: jewellery-hoard association and a clipped inscription

M-294 has a credible association with a jewellery hoard that the original report
interprets as probable goldsmith property. This is useful archaeological evidence.
It does not establish that the room was an operating workshop, that the clipped
seal preserves its entire original inscription, or that its fish sign means a
gemstone. Those are three additional inferences requiring separate evidence.

## The claim and exact artifact

The [2025 bulletin](../data/raw/irc_2025_fish_bulletin.pdf), printed pp8–10
(PDF14–16), argues that House VIII's small room was certainly a jeweller's shop,
that M-294's short text survived clipping intact, and that the middle fish sign
therefore refers to a precious commodity or craft. The last inference also
depends on the author's proposed division into tax/commodity/ending functions.
Those functions are hypotheses, not independently established readings.

The [CISI photograph](../results/primary_pages/m294-cisi-107.png), printed p71,
shows both seal and impression. The [metadata](../results/primary_pages/m294-cisi-404.png),
printed p368, gives Finnish1376, excavation HR4212e, museum ASI63.10.378 and
photograph HU448. Finnish1376 is not treated as a Mahadevan text identifier.
The visible forms are compatible with the secondary transcription below;
precise Wells variant classification is not certified by this image review.

## What the original find can establish

The [independent room review](M294_ROOM_CONTEXT_REVIEW.md) follows the original
Marshall1931 descriptions and separates the deposit, room use, and ownership
interpretation. The find lay immediately beneath the floor of Room8 in HouseVIII.
The original description includes seal376 with the jewellery and other finds.
The volumeII discussion calls the assemblage Hoard3 and infers probable goldsmith
ownership from waste metal and rolled-up ornaments apparently retained for
remelting. These observations support a coherent cache and an occupational
connection. They do not require the cache's location to be a production room.

The original volumes agree on Block2, whereas the secondary row's block/house
field says `1VIII`. The raw export is preserved, with the discrepancy recorded.
The source's dating of the jewellery from the chamber's period is also an
archaeological inference, not an independently dated inscription. “Chert scraper”
is the original wording; the bulletin's “chart scraper” appears to be a typo.

A goldsmith could possess a seal bearing a name, institution, office, religious
formula, transaction category or commodity. These are alternatives compatible
with the association, not proposed translations. The cache supplies no independent
ancient label that identifies which sign carries which kind of information.
Neither a tax rate nor a commodity word follows from the occupation alone.

## Does the short parallel prove completeness?

The [independent photograph review](M294_COMPLETENESS_REVIEW.md) distinguishes
surviving signs from the unknowable lost edge. A coherent short inscription is
plausible, but the photograph and selected parallels cannot certify its original
length. Original and impression are mirror images: clipping is at original-right
and impression-left, beside the ending-shaped sign. A presumed phrase-ending
sign does not establish that this lost margin contained no additional material.
Nothing here establishes that an additional sign actually was lost.

The [saved protocol](../data/processed/m294_protocol.json) was fixed before new
counts, after seeing the author's examples and M-294's photograph. This is an
exploratory source-exposed audit. The [script](../scripts/audit_m294.py) searches
2,914 existing strict secondary rows for literal contiguous sequences. It never
merges the two ending codes or crosses row/uncertainty boundaries.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| W740 W220 W003 (M-294) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| W520 W220 W003 (M-1912) | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

The five exact first-sequence rows are M-294, M-1439, M-1441, M-1442 and M-1443.
The latter four report complete preservation and Fair condition, across tablet
and seal types. They show why a three-sign reconstruction is reasonable.
The longer first-sequence rows are H-1056, H-68, K-9, M-725 and M-1429.
All five have extra file-left signs; four also have extra file-right signs.
For reported right-to-left reading those are respectively suffix and prefix
extensions. M-1672 has the M-1912 sequence plus three reading-prefix signs.
Thus the local pattern can occur within a longer text too.
Suffix extensions are the geometrically relevant caution for the observed
clipped edge. Prefix counts are supplementary comparisons, not evidence of
damage on the stroke side. All direction labels rely on the export's R/L field.

These corpus witnesses have not all been newly checked against photographs.
The [complete output](../results/m294_audit.json) preserves their literal text,
identity, direction and condition. The secondary source calls M-294's text
complete while calling the object a fragment in Poor condition. Inclusion in
the existing strict set inherits that editorial completeness judgment; it does
not independently verify it. M-1912 and M-294 also have different literal ending
codes, even though the paper groups them functionally.

## Result and next discriminating evidence

The positive result is a verified object identity and a substantial association
with probable goldsmith property. The short-message restoration remains plausible
and unproven. The claim that the context determines fish = gemstone remains
unidentified: the semantic allocation, seal function, and original message length
are all additional assumptions.

A useful next test would join independently classified goldsmith and comparison
deposits to complete photographed inscriptions, with deposit type and period
kept separate. An independently readable name, label, contents record or bilingual
would constrain sign meanings more directly. Recounting these selected parallels
would not provide that missing anchor. No established semantic or phonetic value
was added to any of the 417 sign dossiers.
