# Chanhudaro deposit comparison: eligibility before new sign counts

28 September 2026. This phase tests whether the surviving excavation record can
support the fish-family / bead-production comparison proposed in the
[evidence-gap review](DECIPHERMENT_EVIDENCE_GAP_REVIEW.md). The earlier square
counts, 9/D examples and 8/E examples have already been seen. This is development
work, not a blinded replication or a preregistration of a previously unseen site.
No new inscription counts will be made until the archaeological eligibility
decision is saved.

The first finite artifact frame is every catalogue row on Mackay's printed
pages 292–293 (Plates LI–LII). Determine explicitly what those pages omit before
calling them the excavation's complete seal inventory. Include broken objects
and objects with no established surviving inscription. Read the architectural
account through printed page 74 for production contexts, and use later technical
discussion only as a separately identified supplement. Search hits are locators;
substantive new claims require page-image confirmation.

Record square, locus, signed elevation, phase evidence and deposit association
separately. Equal square or locus does not prove equal floor, occupation or
deposition episode. Do not map elevation mechanically to phase. Retain each
contradiction instead of choosing the version that improves the association.

Exposure classes are independently reported bead production, archaeologically
informative comparison without reported bead production, and unknown. The
comparison class is a reporting category, not proof that bead production was
absent. Explicit unfinished beads, drilling waste, drills in an assemblage,
blanks and working surfaces can support production; finished beads alone cannot.
Record shell and metal working, preservation, disturbance and reporting detail
as rival explanations. Assess whether negative evidence is informative given
the excavation and publication method; do not require metaphysical certainty
of absence, and do not equate narrative silence with an informative negative.

Join primary objects to secondary metadata by exact normalized excavation number
within Chanhudaro and compatible object class. Case/space/prefix normalization
must be explicit; suffixes must not be discarded. Multiple matches stay
ambiguous, and conflicting metadata stay visible. No substring-only joins.
The secondary export is not the sampling frame for primary fragments.

A feasible association test needs at least two independently classified exposure
classes with comparable phase and recovery information, secure artifact-to-
deposit joins, and enough independent deposits to separate a craft contrast
from a single household. Positive floors and an unclassified remainder do not
meet that gate. If the gate fails, save its concrete causes and do not calculate
a craft-specific p-value. An exploratory association with reporting categories
may be described only if both categories are meaningfully defined first.

If the gate passes, keep the existing P050, P051, P056–P064 family and W220-only
sensitivity unchanged. A confidently observed member is positive; unreadable
or incomplete text without one is unknown. Include all archaeological seal
finds and bound unknown texts in both directions. Use deposits as sampling
units; group known die/mould relationships and exact text duplicates for
sensitivity. Compare shell/metal contexts and frequency-matched non-fish signs.
Any second sector chosen after seeing its inscriptions remains development
data. Neither a positive association nor its absence establishes a word,
phonetic value, language, or a common meaning for every family member.

The all-sign inventory remains 417 rows with no established semantic or phonetic
values. This phase must yield a usable external-context test or a precise
account of why the surviving record cannot identify that test; collecting more
unjoined examples alone is not success.
