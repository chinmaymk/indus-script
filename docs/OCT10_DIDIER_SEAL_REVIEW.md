# Three native seal views in Didier Figure10b

Research date:2026-10-10. **No secure new fish-family occurrence is established.** All three displayed views remain U for the fixed family, and U for their complete original inscriptions. This does not claim the signs are absent.

The author manuscript's PDF23/printed989 Figure10b contains three seal views. Its caption attributes the objects to chantier2,2016–2018. PDF20/printed986 reports four steatite seals and mentions zebu, gaur and unicorn decoration. The three photographs must not be silently equated with a complete four-object inventory. No individual excavation number or exact deposit is provided on these pages.

The native embedded1235×1446 JPEG is materially clearer than Clio's small assemblage image, but the seal faces themselves remain small:

- Left: an elongated fragment preserves paired legs with hoof-like terminals, curved belly and a tail-like line. The uneven upper edge is broken; no intact sign band is authenticated.
- Middle: a near-square face displays bovine anatomy with elongated and diagonal incisions above it. Some boundaries among horns, inscription and surface texture remain uncertain. No complete fish diagnostic is secure.
- Right: a trapezoidal fragment has crowded incisions and extensive loss on its right side. Neither complete sign identification nor whole-text absence is supported.

The left fragment's distinctive break outline, paired legs and belly agree with the Clio2017 pictured fragment. This strongly supports a same-object candidate and clarifies the formerly ambiguous grooves as animal anatomy. It is not an excavation-number authentication. Do not count the two publications as independent seals.
