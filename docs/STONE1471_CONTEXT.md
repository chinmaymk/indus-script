# Physical context of M77 1471

M77 1471 can now be identified as **HR 2240 = MIC figures 471, 472 and 473 = CISI 1 M-331 (NMI 155)**. This is one object illustrated on several faces. The original excavation report describes it as a square, bossless seal. It does not establish a weight, die, counter, or numerical measurement standard.

## Evidence chain

Mahadevan 1977 appendix V, printed p816/PDF825, explicitly maps MIC volume III plates CIII–CXV figures 1–560 onto refs1001–1560. It also says that when different figures illustrate different sides of one object, the first number denotes the whole object. Marshall 1931 volume II, printed p377/PDF28, explicitly identifies figures 471–473 as different views of the same seal: the first two are obverse/reverse impressions, the last represents two sides. Upper and lower edges are described as blank.

The table on printed p405/PDF56 has three rows, 471, 472 and 473, each with the same excavation number **HR 2240**. The rows give **0.7 × 0.7 inches**, **9 feet 0 inches below surface**, **type C**, and **white steatite**. These are repeated entries for one object, not three independent finds. Thickness and mass are not given. The report's class-C discussion on pp372–373 calls these square seals without a boss and separately mentions HR2240's inscriptions on both main faces and two sides.

CISI 1 printed p368/PDF404 gives **M-331 — HR 2240 — NMI 155 — HU 117**. Its preceding concordance column has1471, but the excavation-number match and original-publication mapping authenticate the identity; a bare integer match between catalogue namespaces would not suffice. CISI 1 printed p84/PDF120 photographs the six surfaces A–F and impressions of the four inscribed surfaces. The modern HR2240/155 marking on E further corroborates the object identity. A has two inscription rows, B and F have sign groups, D has three upright strokes; C and E carry no ancient inscription visible in these photographs. This supports four inscribed surfaces and five lines on one object, without supplying a reading order across surfaces or a semantic interpretation.

## A discrepancy that remains visible

The report's p372 explicitly includes HR2240 among five exceptions to pierced class-C objects. Yet p373's subsequent discussion of perforation and wear ends with a parenthetical list including figures471 and472. The photographs show no obvious perforation. The list may refer more broadly to the seal class rather than only to pierced examples; its scope is ambiguous. We retain the explicit unpierced statement as a reported claim and flag the apparent inconsistency instead of treating a hole as established. The discrepancy does not affect the excavation-number or multi-face identity joins.

## Implications for numeral hypotheses

The object supplies an independently photographed setting for the three-stroke, four-stroke and arranged five-stroke forms under study. Their coexistence does not by itself establish cardinal values, addition, account totals, capacity, or weight. There is no authenticated mass, numbered commodity count, stated capacity, ancient translation, or fixed reading order in this source chain. The label “seal” is the excavator's classification; the photographs and dimensions alone do not demonstrate the complete ancient use.

The published depth is relative to the excavation surface, not an absolute date. The HR prefix locates the object within the Mohenjodaro excavation's HR catalogue/area; no exact room, house, block, or stratigraphic phase was authenticated in this bounded search. No date is inferred from9ft alone.

- Located Marshall volume II through the Harappa bibliographic entry, then downloaded the public Internet Archive metadata, OCR, scan metadata and searchable PDF to `data/raw/`.
- OCR located the exact mentions; original page images were inspected before accepting the table values. The scan metadata contains leaf numbers but no printed-page map, so the searchable PDF supplied the locators.
- A web search for HR2240 and Mohenjodaro returned irrelevant legislative records and supplied no extra archaeological context. These were not downloaded or used as evidence.
- The original MIC volume III plate photograph was not recovered in this bounded pass. The later CISI photographic publication supplies the six-surface views. Its photographs are not a new autopsy or a newly independent linguistic transcription.
