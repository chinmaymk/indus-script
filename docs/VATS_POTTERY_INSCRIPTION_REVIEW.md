# Vats on pottery inscriptions: original pages286–287

**These pages do not identify the inscriptions of jars5919 or10252.** They do provide explicit groups of stamped impressions and distinguish physical features from proposed authorship and meaning. The Archive page map was checked before retrieval: leaves312/313 map to printed286/287, and both printed numbers were then verified in the original images.

Vats describes incision “before or after the wares were finished” and separately discusses engraving “before firing.” He supposes potters made the latter marks and suggests owners applied names or marks in other cases. These are interpretations of agency and meaning, not decoded names. Calling the stamps potters' seals is similarly qualified. [Original p.286 discussion](../data/raw/pottery-text-vats-p286-discussion.png); [p.287 continuation](../data/raw/pottery-text-vats-p287-discussion.png).

The concrete surface observation is that inscriptions on illustrated items1,3,10,12,26,28,48,62,84,85 cut through red or black slip. That supplies a reported order between coating and incision. It does not, by itself, provide independently demonstrated post-firing dates for every example. The last cited item is **85**, not the86 in the retained OCR. No current physical examination of the objects was performed.

The inscription section refers to PlatesCII–CIV, illustrations1–53 and58–85. Its explicit associations are:

| Source references | Reported relationship | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Illustrated21,23,24,46 | Each bears a different rectangular stamp, reported0.9×0.4in | No excavation identifiers supplied here |
| Illustrated53,72,73; unillustrated7860,10589 | Vats assigns them the same stamp, reported0.6×0.35in | Shared impression, not identity of the vessels;7860/10589 are explicitly blurred and unillustrated |
| Illustrated74,76 and unspecified others | Another shared, single-character stamp, reported0.5in square | No named maker, exact list of all specimens, or target-jar join |
| J97 | Unillustrated vessel with an inscription near its bottom, described as upside down | No drawing or sign sequence supplied |

A footnote describes the impression on illustrated73 as upside down. This is an object-specific orientation statement, not permission to reverse all corpus strings. Other placement examples concern middle, rim or smaller-vessel locations; none identifies jar5919 or10252. Illustration numbers must not be conflated with excavation identifiers or TableI serial numbers. In particular, decorative fragments35,36,44 associated with5384 in the preceding p.286 section do not identify TableI jar35/5919.

During this review the parent identified a relevant passage on [Vats p.91](../data/raw/capacity-context-vats-leaf117.jpg), which was also visually checked here: goblet fragment7856 from M12/7 is compared with PlateCIV73. The statement establishes similarity, not that7856 was stamped by the same seal as53/72/73/7860/10589. The parent's separate TableI row44 review concerns the register7856 attached to a large jar containing goblet fragments; whether this is an assemblage-level registration or another source discrepancy is not resolved here.
