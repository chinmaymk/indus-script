# Independent review of the 6640-501 jar inscription

**The rim drawing provisionally supports four visible linear traces, but does not
establish four numerical units.** The jar's body also has non-simple graffiti.
Neither the stroke count nor the vessel capacity alone identifies a unit.

This review concerns Kenoyer, *The Origin, Context and Function of the Indus
Script: Recent Insights from Harappa*, printed p.156/PDF21, Figure7 item2.
The [page render](../results/primary_pages/h95-profile-21.png) was inspected,
followed by the native embedded figure and a nearest-neighbor enlargement of its
rim inset. The [ledger](../data/processed/h95_inscription_review.json) records the
source hash, native dimensions, crop coordinates and individual observations.

The drawing labels the vessel **H95-2661/6640-501**. The caption instead gives
**H95-2662 / 6640-501**. This discrepancy is unresolved. The shared 6640-501 and
Figure7 item2 locate the depicted jar; neither 2661 nor 2662 should be silently
repaired, and these should not be counted as two separate vessels.

## Rim count and image limits

The curved inset above the neck contains what I provisionally count as four long,
oblique traces: two close together on the left, then two further traces to the
right. The last is partly interrupted in this reproduction. The source caption
does not supply a number.

The native figure is only **696 × 803 pixels**, and the marked portion of the rim
occupies approximately **30 × 20 pixels**. Enlargement clarifies the available
pixels without recovering the original engraving. In particular, the close pair
could represent two marks or the edges of a broader incision. An original
photograph or a better original drawing is needed to distinguish those possibilities.
Any capacity-per-mark calculation should therefore make four marks an explicit
scenario and include a three-mark sensitivity; neither is an established numeral
value.

## Body marks and firing stage

The displayed body graffiti include a separate slanting line, a central crossed
or branching group, and a right angular/branching group near the shoulder.
These are spatial descriptions, not sign segmentation or translations. They
show that the complete body inscription cannot simply be treated as parallel
tally strokes.

A small separate upper-body mark is identified by a potter's-mark arrow. The
base inset and lower arrows identify further potter's marks, whose exact shapes
and number are unclear at this resolution.

The source explicitly distinguishes:

- **Post-firing:** graffiti on the rim and body.
- **Pre-firing:** potter's marks on the body and base.

That distinction is the excavator's published attribution, not something this
line-drawing inspection can independently establish from material traces. It
does not establish whether the graffiti recorded capacity, contents, ownership,
transactions, or something else. Nor does their placement on one jar establish
that the rim and body marks were made in the same episode or formed one numerical
expression. No Wells/Mahadevan code, phonetic value, or semantic reading is
assigned here.
