# A primary-supported comb-orientation counterpart

Marshall seal158 (excavationDM50) and Lothal L-89 carry compatible five-group
texts with different comb orientations. Their surviving impressions do not show
the gross corner loss that weakened the L-9 comparison. This strengthens
W526/W527 as a candidate orientation variant; it does not prove identical
sounds, meanings or free interchangeability.

The [original-source review](M934_PRIMARY_REVIEW.md) authenticates MIC158=DM50
using Marshall volumeII printed403/PDF54, and its impression in volumeIII
retained Archive leaf191 (Roman plate heading not certified). The secondary row3329.1 labelled M-934
has concatenated fieldDM50158 and text W526 W555 W235 W002 W861.
The CISI Pakistan-volume metadata for that label remains unchecked; call this
an authenticated MIC158/DM50 object with a qualified secondary CISI alias.

The parent independently checked Mahadevan's appendix printed818/PDF827:
MIC volumeIII items1–560 correspond respectively to references1001–1560.
This explicit catalogue rule identifies MIC158 with text1158. It is not a
numerical suffix guess. The normalized line1158 is printed43/PDF54; its
candidate reading-order sequence is M267 M099 M065 M222 M254, matching
the primary line's five forms. These pages supplement the agent's narrower
review, which correctly left the Mahadevan alias outside its verified scope.

L-89 was independently joined to text7090 through excavation15337. Both
Mahadevan lines normalize to the same five codes. Their Wells sequences
have identical following four tokens, with W526 versus W527 in the comb
position. The original impressions support that orientation difference.
MIC158 is a square animal-bearing seal; L-89 is a rectangular text-only seal.
They are separate objects, not mirrored illustrations of one artifact.

This is evidence for a particular graphic variation within a matching context.
It does not establish that every W526 is an intentional reversal rather than
damage, nor that W526 and W527 have the same function in every inscription.
L-9 remains a damaged witness; M-63 provides another surviving reversed comb
in a different text. A same-context substitution may reflect allography, a
meaningful contrast, or differing normalization/transcription decisions. There
is no bilingual or independently known referent to decide among these.
