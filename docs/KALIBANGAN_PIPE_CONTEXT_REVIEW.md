# Kalibangan pipes 21582 and 21583: independent context audit

Reviewed 2026-09-25. This is a bounded primary-source check of the bead-storage proposal in Mukhopadhyay's IRC Bulletin 6 (2025), §7. It does not assign any semantic or phonetic value.

The original excavation reports establish two inscribed terracotta pipes from a late residential context, and a mechanically compatible joint. They do not, in the reviewed entries, establish recovered beads, a bead-making installation associated with the pipes, end closures, residue, transport packaging, or incision after firing. The proposed gemstone-bead contents therefore remain a hypothesis inferred partly from the proposed sign meanings; they are not an independently known object label against which those meanings can be tested.

## Findings

1. **Identity is explicit.** Part 1 p.556 assigns the fish-marked pipe in illustration 9.91 no.2 to registration **21582**, and the three connected ovals in no.1 to **21583**, by the order of its “respectively” clause. The original refers the former to Mahadevan sign59 and compares the latter with signs415 and417; that comparison is not an exact assignment to either. The photograph on p.555 has the same two marked pipe bodies and labels. Its caption is printed “Pl.9.91”, whereas the prose calls it a figure.

2. **Context is residential and late, with a primary caution.** Part 1 p.74 discusses Group B house remains in ZA8/ZB8/ZB9, successive phases, and two inscribed pipes assigned to Phase V that were **not in situ**. Part 2 p.1060 narrows the stated find context to KLB-1, trench ZA8, quadrant3, a large house associated by the excavators with an important person, Phase V/late Period II. The owner's importance is an archaeological interpretation, not independent identification of a merchant or lapidary. No exact floor elevation, field association of contents, or event of use accompanies these entries. The Group B plan (p.77) shows structures across phases III–V and does not label the pipes' exact findspots. Surrounding drains and bathing platforms do not prove these particular pipes were installed in them.

3. **The joint is real evidence of compatibility.** Part 2 describes wheel manufacture, one intact and one slightly damaged example, a projecting rim fitting into the other pipe, and a joined length of 56 cm. It gives a pipe length of 26 cm up to the rim, rim width 4 cm, rim diameter 11.8 cm, and diameter 14.5 cm at the other end. Separate and joined studio photographs illustrate the pieces. The chain-marked piece has the projecting collar; the fish-marked piece sits over it in the published joined view. This supports a functional socket joint. The text and photographs do **not explicitly establish that the two were excavated already joined**, that their marked faces retained an original alignment, or that the pictured configuration preserves their last ancient use. “Not in situ” likewise does not demonstrate they had never been installed, had been repurposed, or had stored any specific content.

4. **Drainage is also an interpretation, not an excavated installation here.** Part 1 cautiously suggests water drainage. Part 2 supplies comparative drainage examples from Mohenjodaro and elsewhere. Its lengthy description of an internally finger-grooved, cream-slipped pipe enclosed in brickwork is a quotation about a **Mohenjodaro** object, not measured use-wear or installation evidence for the two Kalibangan objects. Neither drainage nor bead storage can be elevated to a directly observed use event for this pair from these entries alone.

5. **Incision timing is unresolved.** The primary entries call the marks inscribed/incised and the pipes well fired. They do not say the marks were cut after firing. Immediately below the pipe entry, Part 1 starts a separate pottery section distinguishing pre-firing from post-firing incision using depth, displaced clay/ridges, and surface scaling, with qualifications. That discussion does not classify either pipe. Part 1 p.558 also acknowledges uncertainty about the purpose of single pre-firing pot signs and offers potters' marks only as a guess. The high-resolution excavation photographs show marks but are insufficient for a confident technological diagnosis. The 2025 assertion that the marks were incised after the pipes were made therefore remains unsupported by the cited pipe descriptions; wheel-forming before inscription would in any event not entail firing before inscription.

6. **Contents and closures remain unreported within the checked evidence.** Neither pipe entry nor the published pipe photographs identifies surviving beads, beads on sticks, textile/leather caps, wooden plugs, clay closures, bindings, organic residue, or a deposit sealed inside. This is an absence of reporting in the checked passages, not proof that all such traces were absent in excavation. The caps, ties, cargo, and protective-container arrangement in the 2025 paper are conjectures. Its explicitly AI-generated illustrations demonstrate an imagined design, not archaeological observations or an independent test of sign meanings. The paper itself describes multiple possible uses, including concealed valuables, so physical feasibility alone cannot select gemstone beads.

## Inferential consequence and next discriminating evidence

The signs demonstrably occur on two compatible pieces from a common reported find context. That is stronger than resemblance between unrelated artifacts, but weaker than a preserved two-sign text on one object or a container recovered with known contents. It can inform sign distribution and object-use hypotheses without fixing meanings. The inference “fish means gemstone, therefore the pipe stored gemstones, therefore fish means gemstone” would reuse its own assumption.

Useful discriminating evidence would be field photographs or original registers recording whether the pieces were touching/joined at discovery, retained sediment or residues with secure sampling history, closure or binding traces, technological inspection of incision edges, and a corpus of comparable pipes with independently established use and marks. A verified original association could support a storage hypothesis; it would still require controls to distinguish gemstone cargo, other cargo, ownership, manufacture, or a broader administrative mark. No established sign values change.
