# Review of the 2026 statistical-specificity critique

**This paper supplies a methodological counterexample, not new Indus sign readings.**
No sign dossier or proposed phonetic value changes as a result of this review.

Nikhil Raghavendra's [arXiv2608.02999v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02999v1), submitted
4August2026, constructs SIGIL, a synthetic system with meanings but no assigned
phonology. Its registry has54 entries, but only **six dependent outcomes are exactly
scored**: one repetition measure, two handedness measures and three lexical-model
fit tests. The paper reports matching those six outcomes; these are not six
independent positive language classifications. Its multilingual decoding experiment
evaluates dictionary segmentation on SIGIL, including grouped test data. It does
not establish correct held-out Indus readings or report semantic-recovery accuracy.
The claimed result concerns statistical sufficiency, not whether Indus encoded
speech. See sections5–7 and the limitations on PDFp9.

The bounded reading is logically useful: a genuine counterexample can disprove
“this statistic necessarily indicates linguistic encoding.” It cannot, without
additional probability models, determine how strongly a statistic favors one
archaeological hypothesis over another. Our existing structural results therefore
remain observations to explain; they do not become evidence that Indus was
nonlinguistic. Good sign prediction, word-list segmentation, sound recovery and
semantic recovery must continue to be reported as different achievements.

## Reproducibility check

I inspected the PDF text and hyperlink inventory, the public arXiv source archive,
and public metadata for an author-linked candidate GitHub account. **No identifiable
SIGIL code/data URL was located.** The source archive contains the manuscript,
typesetting/bibliographic files and27 figure PDFs, not the generator, corpus,
decoder or evaluation registry. The36 returned repository names/descriptions did
not identify the advertised release. This is a bounded negative search, not proof
that no release exists elsewhere. No third-party code was executed.

Until a versioned release is located, this review cannot independently substantiate
the reported numerical outcomes or inspect whether training isolation is implemented
as described. A future replication should freeze the corpus, generator and registry;
verify exact decision rules separately from reconstructed measures; and reproduce
the lexical experiment's train/test split and optimization stages. A successful
replication would still provide a comparison system, not an Indus translation key.
