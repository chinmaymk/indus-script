# Jar 3822: original text, photograph and catalogue joins

Independent bounded review, 2026-09-27. The retained original establishes excavation jar **3822 → Vats Plate XXIII e**. The suggested panel c is incorrect. No CISI, FC or M77 reference is authenticated in this review, and no sign sequence or meaning is assigned.

## Original identity and publication

Vats volume I, printed pp.254–255, Table I row18 (retained leaves280–281), explicitly gives register3822, MoundF, TrenchI, squareM12/6, depth15ft9in, StratumVI. It calls the jar large, napiform and in sherds. The printed measurements are height2ft3in and mouth diameter11in; these are retained as the author's measurements without deriving capacity.

The continuation of the SAME row on p255 explicitly reports three pictograms at the shoulder, two nearby jar-stands3823A/B, and PlateXXIII **e**. The serial18 at the right edge anchors this continuation to row18 at the left edge of p254. These three pictograms are the excavator's reported count; they are not an independently transcribed three-sign sequence.

PlateXXIII, retained plates PDF page58, panel e at bottom left, captions an inscribed jar and jar-stands of StratumVI in squareM12/6. The register number is supplied by the explicit row18 cross-reference, not by a number visibly painted on the photograph. This is a secure publication join. The plate caption alone does not label which stand is3823A or3823B, and this review does not identify them separately by shape.

Panel c instead captions southern-section rectangular hearths and circular mangers in squareM12/19. It is excluded. The searchable Vats OCR has a second locator in narrative printed p104: number3822 and PlateXXIIIe. Since that narrative page was not separately visually authenticated in this pass, it remains corroborating OCR location information, not an additional primary visual pin.

## What the photograph actually permits

Panel e shows one large jar with a comparatively short neck and broad shoulder beside the jar-stands. A conspicuous irregular boundary/crack crosses the upper right shoulder; other surface bands and weak traces are present. These features agree with the report's fragmented preservation, but the photograph cannot distinguish every break, restoration edge or incision.

The published scene is not a close-up of the inscription. The saved native-pixel shoulder crop does not permit secure isolation, stroke counts, direction or catalogue identification of three individual glyphs. This review therefore records `reported_pictograms=3`, `photo_verified_sign_count=null`, and no ordered transcription. It would be an overclaim to recover three specific signs from the available shoulder image or treat the visible irregular line as a sign.

## Bounded catalogue search and its limits

- CISI1 basic-data printed372/PDF408, including the Harappa graffiti rowsH370–H382, was visually inspected: no explicit3822 field or XXIIIe bridge was found there. H370–372 retain their separate identities from the preceding review; none is substituted for3822.
- CISI2 basic-data printed444–445/PDF479–480 was inspected for an explicit field/plate bridge; none was located. This is a bounded lookup, not proof that the object is absent from every CISI volume or archive. Sparse whole-PDF text extraction makes its negative string searches weak evidence.
- M77 AppendixV printed824–825/PDF833–834 pottery-graffiti metadata was visually inspected. It lists other excavation numbers and photograph/publication references; no3822 or VatsXXIIIe bridge was found. The nearby13823 entry belongs to the miniature-tablet section and is not3822. Exact string search in retained M77 OCR also gave no3822 hit; OCR absence alone proves nothing.
- The retained secondary CSV has an H98-3822 field (H-1726), H98-3823 (H-1761), and13823 (H-966). These are distinct complete identifiers, not alternative spellings of Vats3822. They are rejected as substring-only locators; no secondary proposed reading is used.
- A CISI1 OCR hit `3823` on PDF407 was inspected against the original page. No corresponding excavation-number3823 entry was confirmed; the nearby H160 row instead reads FC3276 and excavation2630. The disordered OCR hit is rejected, rather than assigned to a jar-stand. The column key also distinguishes FC from excavation numbers.

The conclusion is **original excavation/publication identity authenticated, catalogue text bridge unresolved**. No equal-number rule, similar-looking three-sign sequence, or capacity expectation has been used to fill that gap.
