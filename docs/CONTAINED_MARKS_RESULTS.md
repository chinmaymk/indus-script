# Marked objects inside vessels and the limits of identifier readings

The follow-up has covered all **five contained-mark or seal-fragment rows** from
the earlier fixed table screen. Only the previously authenticated faience object
inside jar10252 has an accepted primary text reference. The four remaining rows
now have explicit identity limits. No missing text has been supplied by matching
a number, a nearby find or a vaguely similar illustration.

A separate primary passage supplies a narrow semantic constraint: two goblets
reportedly shared a stamp. **That stamp design alone could not uniquely identify
each individual vessel.** This does not identify what the design did represent.

## A shared stamp constrains uniqueness

Vats volumeI printed91 reports trough470 in squareM12/13 containing **two
pointed-base goblets, each stamped with the same seal**, referring to PlateCII21.
The parent and an independent reviewer read the original page; the
[source-and-logic review](REPEATED_GOBLET_STAMP_REVIEW.md) preserves an exact crop.
The author's suggestion that this was a potter's name is explicitly conjectural.
No individual goblet field numbers or sign transcription are recovered here.

The conditional proof is short. Let A and B be the two distinct vessels described
by the source, and let D(v) be the shared stamp design on vessel v. The report
gives A≠B and D(A)=D(B). Therefore D is not one-to-one on individual vessels.
A proposed reading in which that design alone uniquely serializes each vessel
is incompatible with this report. This is a source-reported counterexample,
conditional on its accurate description; no modern examination of both objects
or fresh independent photograph of each has been performed.

Owner, maker, workshop, batch or class identifiers remain possible because any
of those can recur across vessels. Additional unreported marks might distinguish
the vessels. The conclusion neither chooses one of these meanings nor rules out
serial identifiers elsewhere in the script.

## Surface features and proposed authorship

The [original pottery discussion](VATS_POTTERY_INSCRIPTION_REVIEW.md), printed
286–287, identifies groups reportedly made with shared stamps and examples whose
incisions cut through red or black slip. Those are statements about graphic
production and surface order. Cutting through slip establishes incision after
that coating, not by itself incision after firing. The passage separately
discusses pre-firing marks, then supposes potter or owner authorship; its proposed
names are not deciphered words.

Its shared-stamp group53/72/73 plus blurred unillustrated7860/10589 is distinct
from the weaker statement that7856 is similar to73. A footnote calls73's impression
upside down; it does not license reversing all corpus strings. Illustration85,
not OCR86, is the final listed slip-cutting example. Neither target jar5919 nor
10252 is identified in this discussion.
