# Older Umma sealing: accession identified, excavation context unverified

The older object catalogued as M77 reference 9811 is the clay sealing published
by Scheil in 1925, not the object reported from the 2017 SAHI work. Parpola's
*Deciphering the Indus Script* (1994), printed p. 113 / PDF p. 131, Fig. 7.16,
identifies the older clay tag as **Ashmolean 1931.120** and illustrates both faces.
The caption and photographs were visually checked in the
[saved page](../results/primary_pages/umma-old-parpola-p113.png). This is a
published accession attribution; an accession-register entry or current official
museum object record was **not recovered** in this audit.

## Identity and source chain

The [existing selection ledger](../data/processed/m77_foreign_selection_review.json)
records 9811 as Djoka (Umma), object code 2 (sealing), citing *Revue d'Assyriologie*
XXII (1925), p. 56. The journal publisher's [issue contents](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i23283221)
locate V. Scheil, “Un nouveau sceau hindou pseudo-sumérien,” at pp. 55–56,
volume 22, **issue 2**, article 23290782. The original two-page article's full text
was not recovered: the PDF request returned a client challenge. Its title does
not establish a Sumerian reading or a bilingual inscription.

An archaeological account by Maurizio Tosi, quoted on
[Harappa](https://www.harappa.com/blog/indus-seal-impression-umma-mesopotamia),
reports dealer delivery to the Louvre before World War I, claimed Jhoka origin,
and transfer to the Ashmolean in 1931. It explicitly treats the claimed findspot
as unconfirmed. We therefore retain **reported antiquities-market provenance**,
not an excavated Umma context. The quoted account is attributed to *Save Mohenjo
Daro!* (2022), pp. 56–57; that reprint and underlying museum acquisition records
were not directly examined. No verified purchase price, seller name, Louvre
inventory number, stratum, associated lot, or find coordinates were recovered.

## What the reverse establishes

The Parpola photograph shows a patterned reverse, captioned as a cloth impression.
Tosi interprets the traces as cloth covering a jar mouth, a surrounding string,
and a knot cavity. **Cloth contact is the better-supported observation; jar
closure is a reported reconstruction.** Neither source supplies identified
contents, a quantified amount, or a fiber analysis establishing cotton. A textile
trace does not translate a sign as “cloth,” “cotton,” or the contents of a package.

The reviewed obverse bears Indus signs and the familiar one-horned animal motif.
The reverse view supplies no recognizable cuneiform text. Neither reviewed
description identifies cuneiform on this object. This supports “no authenticated
same-object bilingual anchor recovered,” not an autopsy-based proof that every
surface is uninscribed. The general cuneiform discussion beneath Fig. 7.16 is
comparative prose, not a transcription of the clay tag.

## Separation and remaining uncertainty

The older published object predates the 2017 find report by decades. Its
1931.120 accession attribution and intact published outline must not be merged
with TJ17-PRO-S56 simply because both are called Umma finds. The modern case's
field evidence is audited separately by the parent investigation. This audit
does not independently authenticate the modern object's number or context.

M77's exported candidate contains five sign codes, whereas the quoted Tosi
account says six signs. No new glyph segmentation was performed, and this
discrepancy remains open. It is not a reason to invent a sixth code or to rewrite
the historical export. No sign value, sound, commodity or personal name is assigned.

## Audit trail

[Structured findings](../data/processed/umma_older_identity.json) distinguish
publication evidence, interpretation and missing records. All downloaded files
and web captures are in `data/raw/` with the prefix `umma_old_`; the

byte counts, SHA256 and retrieval date. Four archive queries did not locate the

these are retained as failed-retrieval evidence, not research documents. Public
web extraction supplied the Tosi account and journal contents. A general museum
search surfaced the unrelated Mohenjo-daro prism EAMd.13; it was rejected as an
identity match. Museum web entry points did not yield the requested object record.

## Addendum: five catalogue units, six-report discrepancy unresolved

On follow-up, the primary M77 row 9811 was inspected directly on printed p.160 /
PDF p.171, together with the numbered sign list on printed pp.32,33,35. Reading
the normalized row right to left gives **M029–M336–M099–M104–M162**, exactly the
five exported codes. This verifies the export against the printed catalogue,
not against an artifact autopsy. See the
[row crop](../results/primary_pages/umma-old-m77-9811-crop.png) and
[complete page](../results/primary_pages/umma-old-m77-p160.png).

The complex figure with two side elements is one numbered sign, M029. The U form
with an internal stem and small terminal lozenge is also one numbered sign,
M336. The two short strokes form M099 and the four-stroke group forms M104;
M162 is the forked form with a descending stem. Thus counting individual strokes
or pictorial components is not equivalent to counting M77 sign units. This gives
a possible class of explanations for differing counts, but there is **no evidence
identifying which grouping, if any, produced Tosi's six**.

The [enlarged obverse](../results/primary_pages/umma-old-obverse-crop.png) preserves
the complex left-hand form and neighboring U-like form, but shadow and the
halftone reproduction make parts of the remaining field difficult to distinguish.
Its apparent left-to-right arrangement is opposite the normalized M77 drawing;
no export reversal or judgment about the original photographic orientation is
made here. Neither a securely separate sixth glyph nor a definite damaged sixth
glyph can be established from this image. The enlargement adds display size,
not new photographic information.

Conclusion: **five is now directly verified as M77's segmentation; six remains
an unexplained report**, not a correction to the catalogue. No token, sign meaning
or reading direction was changed. The primary sign-list images viewed were
`sign_list_page_32.png`, `sign_list-044.png`, and `sign_list-046.png`; the intervening
`sign_list-045.png` was also inspected while locating the U variants. The local
five numbered PNG glyphs were supplemental comparison aids.
