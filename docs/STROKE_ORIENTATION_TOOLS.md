# Stroke orientation on two metal tools

The original excavation report supports a vertical/horizontal contrast on the knife DK7800 **in prose**. On DK7857 the original drawing shows four upright marks in a tilted patch above seven upright marks. Pettersson labels a tilted row as `4t`; this drawing does not contradict that notation merely because the individual strokes are upright. Our earlier contrary attribution is withdrawn; see the [source correction](PETTERSSON_ORIENTATION_ATTRIBUTION.md). Neither object supplies a new numerical mass anchor.

The proposed starting locators, CXXVI items 1 and 4, were incorrect. Mackay's catalogue p.489 identifies them as spear-head DK7858 and axe DK7853. They show no inscription patches on the reviewed drawing. The target objects instead occur at **CXXVII1** and **CXXXIII1**. This correction concerns the starting search request, not an error inferred in the previous CXXVI2/3/5 audit.

These are different find contexts. The knife's house VI is a ditto in the catalogue, inherited from the preceding row. Level figures are decimal feet, not feet-and-inches notation. The copper classification and tool names are reported catalogue descriptions, not new material analyses or functional determinations.

## What the drawing and photographs resolve

On CXXVII1, both drawn groups contain upright marks. The upper four-mark patch tilts diagonally, while the lower seven-mark patch is almost horizontal. Group layout and individual stroke orientation are distinct. Our first comparison incorrectly treated Pettersson’s row label as requiring individually transverse strokes. The drawing contains no additional complex sign. Its three round perforations are object features, not assigned signs. The photographs show the damaged, folded blade; this scan does not resolve all eleven marks sufficiently to independently settle the original incisions. The row-label notation itself is compatible with the drawing.

![Native crop of the two drawn groups on CXXVII1](../results/primary_pages/stroke-orientation-spear-marks-native.png)

The knife photograph shows corrosion, a long tang with a perforation, and marks on the blade. Its low contrast does not independently verify every mark named in the prose. No additional complex sign is securely identified, but absence cannot be proved from this photograph. The `4i 4t` description thus has original textual support; repeating that description in a later publication adds no second object observation.

The plate list p.viii identifies CXXVII as drawings and CXXXI as photographs. CXXXIII1 is a published object photograph. No new autopsy, surface measurement, or independent high-resolution image was obtained. These observations were made after the later counts were known, not in a blinded test.

## Dimensions and missing masses

DK7857 is tabulated on p.489 as 11.4 in long, 3.1 in wide, with tang thickness 0.11 in and blade thickness 0.1 in. The prose p.460 calls the length restored and width estimated, and gives greatest thickness near the tang as 0.13 in. The table/prose thickness difference is retained, not silently harmonized. The restored outline and missing material prevent a reliable original mass reconstruction from those dimensions.

DK7800 is tabulated on p.493 as 4.95 in long, 1.51 in wide, with tang thickness 0.12 in and blade thickness 0.09 in. Page 463 says it was badly corroded. No numerical mass is supplied for this knife in the reviewed passages.

Page 454 explicitly says weighing CXXVII1 was useless because the thin blade had suffered from oxidation. The numerical masses on that page belong to CXXVI2,3,5. They must not be reassigned to the spear-head or knife. Neither the stated dimensions nor the shared visual stroke family establishes a mass unit, a numerical base, equal values across orientations, or a reading for other Indus signs.
