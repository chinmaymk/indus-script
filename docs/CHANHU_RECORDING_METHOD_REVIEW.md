# What a Chanhudaro locus number records

28 September 2026. Mackay's original preface, printed vi–vii, was visually
checked in retained scans [vi](../data/raw/chanhu-methods-leaf10.jpg) and
[vii](../data/raw/chanhu-methods-leaf11.jpg). These two pages were downloaded
successfully; response-header records and retrieval metadata are preserved.

The numbering system followed spatial positions down through successive
excavation levels. Mackay explicitly contrasts the alignments of successive
buildings at Chanhudaro with those at Mohenjo-daro and describes keeping the
numbered positions fixed while removing strata. Thus an identical locus number
is not, by itself, an identical archaeological deposit. This is a constraint
from the recording method, in addition to the observed conflicts among objects.
The excavator's statement that positional deviations were small does not make
the occupations contemporary.

The survey used 50-foot squares. The working datum was 100.45 feet above the
reported sea-level reference, five feet above the later-correlated local
benchmark. Catalogue levels are relative elevations, not depths below a uniform
surface or direct chronological labels. Their signs must be retained.

The preface also describes field cards recording each find's locus and level
before removal, followed by completion of the record in the workroom. It reports
about 5,000 cards/finds. These are not 5,000 seals, and that number is not an
excavation-wide count of individual beads or of deposits. A footnote states
that some finds judged unimportant were reburied after inspection. Neither
statement establishes that any particular unlisted bead or seal was discarded.
The pages do not provide recovery rates, systematic screening data, or a complete
deposit-by-material inventory.

The field cards, if extant and accessible, are a concrete next source for
resolving conflicting find levels and reconstructing comparable assemblages.
Their present location, completeness and availability have not been established
by this page review. The illustrated catalogue remains a published selection,
not a substitute for that full field-record archive.

No inscription strings were inspected for this review. These observations
change how provenance may be joined; they assign no sign meanings.
