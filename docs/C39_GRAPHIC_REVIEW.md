# C39 graphic and catalogue review

C39 supports a longitudinal arrangement of angular and U-like forms, but this scan does not independently establish an exact, complete `XXUUU` transcription. The notation is Pettersson’s proposed reading. Its major spatial features are compatible with the photograph; uncertainty about faint strokes and segmentation prevents treating five tokens as an observed count.

## Sources and object identity

The retained CISI I photograph is printed p337, PDF page373: C39 A(1), labelled33%, and A(2), labelled100%. Its p375/PDF411 row identifies C39 as Finnish5083, CH2529H, ASI74.1.48. Photo references are A(1) Yale1982.10:31–32 and A(2) Yale1981.13:10–12. These are source metadata, not dates of inscription discovery. The original excavation publication’s CE LXXIV.2 join was reviewed separately in `C40_ORIGINAL_REVIEW.md`.

This audit used the existing retained PDF and page renders, with no downloads. The accompanying JSON pins twelve primary PDFs and page/crop images by size and SHA256. It records all crop coordinates and attempts. This is a manual review conducted after learning Pettersson’s reading, not a blinded reading or an autopsy.

## What is visible

The principal region includes triangular/double-triangle-like contours above several curved U-like incisions. A narrow angular waist and a horizontal triangular edge are conspicuous. Two angular complexes are plausible, but weak connecting strokes and uncertain boundaries prevent independently authenticating two identical sign tokens. Three U-like curves below are also plausible; the lower contour is faint. Their openings generally face toward the angular region in the printed orientation. This supports the relative spatial relation more strongly than it supports an exact count.

Thin diagonal and curving marks are also visible above the principal cluster. Further shallow curved or horizontal marks appear below it. We cannot distinguish additional ancient characters from scratches, corrosion or other surface effects. These marks therefore remain unclassified: neither counted as additional signs nor silently removed to obtain the expected five.

A narrow horizontal line crosses the whole A(2) image, including its background. A(1) also shows a light band around the object. That line is excluded from glyph counting. Its material and purpose are not established here.

The full A(2) crop and three overlapping native-pixel inspection crops preserve the field. No rotation, contrast adjustment, tracing, interpolation or retouching was used. “Above” and “below” describe the printed image and imply no reading direction.

## Attribution and limits

Pettersson1999 p91/PDF3 proposes `XXUUU`, specifies that the U openings face the Xs, and describes X as a double triangle with a citation to Parpola sign234. The numbered sign equivalence has not been independently verified here. Pettersson makes the numeral comparison conditional and excludes this object from his calculations because its mass is unreported and the proposed X numeral differs from the other specimens. Neither the photograph nor this audit establishes a numerical value, a weight equation, a reading direction or an equivalence across M/W/P sign inventories.

## Bounded M77 coverage check

Mahadevan1977 Appendix V, printed p825/PDF834, contains the complete Chanhudaro subsection. Its CE list explicitly includes LXXIV.1 as6306, giving a useful positive control. It does not list LXXIV.2. The other-item rows do not supply CH2529H or ASI74.1.48 either. Thus no C39-to-M77 join is established by this bounded lookup.

This is not an exhaustive search for alternate attributions throughout M77 and does not prove global omission. The 1981/1982 photo references are not used to infer absence from a1977 catalogue. No replacement M77 identifier has been assigned.

## Recorded outcome

The exact five-token reading remains an attributed, visually plausible candidate. The central angular/curved arrangement and its relative orientation are better supported than a complete count. Extra surface marks remain unresolved. The local M77 source-join check supplies no positive C39 mapping. These limits should accompany any subsequent numerical or corpus test.
