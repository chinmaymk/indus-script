# Renewed external-anchor search: a recovered image, no authenticated reading

The new result is an inspectable reproduction of the modern Umma object.
Independent visual review confirms two relatively clear upper forms, while
damage and small image size prevent a complete transcription. This closes part
of an image-access gap. It supplies no name, commodity, count or parallel text
that could establish a sign value.

The four initial branches each used five targeted queries: Chanhudaro public
records, recent primary publications, inscribed weights and Umma report
recovery. A further three-query follow-up pursued the one new residue-study
lead. The limits make the search reproducible; they do not make it exhaustive.
Every returned source used in the reviews, retained image/PDF, response header
and web-tool capture is kept in data/raw. The detailed branch reports preserve
exact queries, failed accesses and source identities.

These distinctions matter for any proposed decipherment. An object's mass is
not necessarily a quantity encoded by its inscription. A photographed sign is
not a known reading. A seal next to a readable tablet in a publication is not
a bilingual. A residue measurement on another vessel at the same site cannot
be assigned to the inscription under study. A recent upload is not necessarily
a recent archaeological discovery.

The useful next documentary targets are specific: the original SAHI p59 and
catalogue identity, individual Penn1064 fieldcards/site-level records, and the
Kotada Bhadli sample photographs or supplements with independently checked
inscriptions. Better images alone would improve sign classification; a meaning
claim additionally needs independent evidence connecting that text to what it
refers to, followed by checks on other objects.

No semantic or phonetic assignments have been added. All417 inventory entries
remain unassigned. These are documented research advances and exclusions,
not a solution to the script. The goal remains unresolved.
