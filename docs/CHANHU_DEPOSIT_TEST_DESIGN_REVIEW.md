# Deposit-and-phase comparison: independent design audit

This review was written before any new sign outcomes were inspected. It uses the existing evidence-gap review, archaeological context review, fish-context audit and frozen family protocol. The sign results already printed in the requested audit are discovery information; they do not become unseen validation data by acquiring better provenance. This review neither changes the P050/P051/P056–P064 family nor computes new sign counts.

**A feasible first test concerns association with independently documented bead-production evidence among report-catalogued seals.** It does not identify the causal effect of an actual workshop, or the sign's meaning. A comparison deposit need not have impossibly perfect evidence that bead production never occurred. It does need archaeological reporting sufficiently informative to distinguish “no diagnostic evidence reported under this inspection rule” from “not described.” That first label is a legitimate observed comparison category, with a reporting-bias sensitivity; it is not verified archaeological absence.

## What is identifiable now

The inspected sources establish that bead production was reported outside square 9/D and that its named building encompasses distinct rooms, floors, fills and adjoining contexts. They support independently defined production-evidence categories and specific object-location joins. They do not yet supply a complete seal-by-deposit table with comparable phase assignments and reporting coverage. Consequently, a deposit contrast cannot presently be calculated from these reviews alone.

The [workshop review](CHANHU_WORKSHOP_CONTEXT_REVIEW.md) establishes these consequential examples:

- Room 215 has unfinished beads on an earthen floor at +9.3 ft, while seal 2285 is in a room-find list at +10.4 ft. A common room supports a contextual association, but those two observations do not by themselves authenticate the same floor event. Neither identical locus nor an arbitrary elevation window repairs this.
- Passage 286 has its own floor association and seal 2428 at +9.7 ft; courtyard 297 has a spread numbered 2529 at about +10.4 ft and seal 2529cc. Adjacency supports an expanded complex analysis but does not make every deposit one independent workshop.
- For locus 174, the seal's narrative level and catalogue level conflict. The two documented alternatives must survive into the assignment sensitivity; choosing the convenient level would be circular.
- The Harappa II beadmaking synthesis names loci 450, 293, 190, 215, 174, 85 and 253. It gives candidate production contexts, not a one-to-one join from its list of squares, nor a phase assignment for every seal found in those squares.
- Seal fragment 2558 has an archaeological record but no established surviving script. An inscribed-object export alone therefore misses part of the target denominator.

Available claims form a ladder: (1) recorded same-deposit association; (2) phase-compatible association with a building or activity area; (3) association with reported production evidence across deposits; (4) association with actual production; (5) a semantic reading. Each higher claim requires assumptions beyond the previous one. The present design can directly address (1)–(3) if the ledger is completed. Imperfect archaeological evidence may inform (4) probabilistically, but cannot establish it without an explicit observation model. None of these alone establishes (5).

## Freeze eligibility before joining signs

Build two ledgers. First enumerate **all report-catalogued seal objects**, including uninscribed-looking fragments, from a declared finite catalogue section. Do not select artifacts through the inscription export or fish examples. Second enumerate all deposits to which those objects might belong and retrieve the same categories of archaeological evidence for every candidate. Keep seals as the primary object class; tags and pottery require separate analyses. This is a report-catalogued frame, not a claim of exhaustive recovery of every ancient seal.

Each record should pass or fail the following gates, with failure recorded rather than deleted:

| Gate | Required record | Treatment of uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Artifact identity | Excavation identifier with source locator, suffixes and plate item where available; explicit duplicate links | Ambiguous identities are candidate sets, not new independent objects. No identity from text similarity alone. |
| Deposit identity | Locus plus named floor, surface, fill, spread or other reported event | A locus/elevation pair without an event interpretation is a provenance observation, not automatically a deposit. Preserve it for the broader tier. |
| Phase compatibility | Source-supported phase for the particular deposit; stratigraphic relation and rationale | Record allowed phases. Do not derive phase from page heading, square or absolute elevation alone. |
| Production evidence | Raw statements and locations of unfinished blanks/beads, drilling waste, tools and working surfaces | Separate direct associations, nearby/same-building evidence and merely regional reports. Isolated finished beads and an author's workshop label alone do not qualify as diagnostic production evidence. |
| Reporting coverage | Whether the account supplies a general assemblage, targeted craft description, catalogue-only entry or no assemblage description | Catalogue-only or silent contexts are unclassified. General assemblage accounts with no diagnostics can be lower-evidence comparators. |
| Preservation and joins | Every framed seal retained, with certainty of family presence/absence left masked until freeze | Missing texts do not disqualify deposits. Unknown outcome later receives bounds, never an automatic negative. |
| Dependence | Same physical object, impression/cast, die, text type, deposit, building/complex | Store separate identifiers at each level; do not flatten them into one “duplicate” flag. |

Use three distinct exposure labels: **D+** = diagnostic production evidence directly associated with the eligible deposit; **D0** = a comparably described eligible assemblage without such evidence reported; **D?** = inadequately described, indirectly associated, contradictory or phase-unresolved. A report of many unfinished beads or a coherent production assemblage qualifies D+; raw material or a single ambiguous tool alone warrants D? unless an independently justified rubric states otherwise. Freeze the rubric and preserve each underlying observation. In a secondary tier, define **associated-area D+** from explicit building/phase association; do not silently promote it to direct D+.

The absence of a hypothetical perfect negative does not stop this observed-evidence contrast. Conversely, inventing a numerical probability that D0 was truly a workshop would add unsupported precision. If archaeological reviewers provide warranted probability ranges, retain their rationale; otherwise use a stated “up to k disputed labels” sensitivity rather than pretending those k values are estimated.

## Concrete finite comparison

The primary question is: **within phase-compatible deposits of comparable deposition and reporting type, is the frozen family more prevalent among seals from D+ than D0 deposits?** This is non-circular when exposure, eligibility, joins and matching are frozen independently of the signs, even though it remains a source-exposed historical analysis.

1. Form strata from source-supported phase, deposit kind (for example floor/spread versus fill) and reporting tier. Collapse categories only under a declared archaeological rule, before seeing outcomes. Never choose bins that maximize overlap or a sign contrast. Report strata with only one exposure group instead of hiding them.
2. Keep the finite matched set containing both D+ and D0. A single pair permits a local descriptive contrast; it supplies no replication across production areas. Multiple floors of one complex do not create independent activity replications. Show the number of connected complexes per group and stratum before unmasking outcomes.
3. Within deposit d, use the family-bearing **physical-seal proportion**, not raw token frequency: p_d = family-bearing objects / all framed seal objects. A certain surviving family member is positive even if the remainder is damaged; absence requires the pre-existing complete/known-text rule. Unknown original text remains unknown. Deposits with no framed seals cannot have a sign proportion and are kept in the frame's zero-seal coverage table.
4. Define the primary contrast as the equally weighted average of within-stratum differences in **mean deposit proportions**. Fix stratum weights to 1/K for the K eligible strata before signs are joined. Report an object-weighted sensitivity separately; the two answer different questions and may disagree.
5. Keep the existing plain-W220 sensitivity distinct. It cannot replace the primary family after results are seen. A bead-specific interpretation additionally needs comparisons against independently coded shell/metal evidence and a predeclared control-sign procedure; a generic workshop contrast alone cannot supply that specificity.

Equal deposit weighting prevents a seal-rich room from dominating the descriptive estimand. It does not make deposits independent or correct differential publication. Do not require an equal *number of finds* in treatment and comparison contexts: activity can itself affect artifact richness, and matching on that consequence may distort the comparison. Match reporting opportunity and deposit kind first; report assemblage richness as a rival explanation or sensitivity, explicitly acknowledging that its adjustment can remove part of an activity association.

The finite enrichment prediction is Delta > 0. A robust nonpositive finite contrast would contradict that direction **in this fixed eligible frame**; it would not disprove all occupational functions in the population. A positive contrast supports an external contextual relationship, not a word or phonetic value. An interval crossing zero leaves the direction unidentified. Do not rescue a failed contrast by changing family, exposure tier or phase boundaries after unmasking.

## Exact uncertainty bounds and their limits

For a fixed set of objects in deposit d, let a_d be certainly positive, b_d certainly absent and u_d unknown, with n_d = a_d+b_d+u_d. Then:

`L_d = a_d/n_d`, `U_d = (a_d+u_d)/n_d`.

For fixed D+/D0 groups in stratum s, the difference of their mean deposit proportions is bounded by:

`L_s = mean_D+(L_d) - mean_D0(U_d)`

`U_s = mean_D+(U_d) - mean_D0(L_d)`.

Apply the frozen nonnegative stratum weights to these limits. These are exact worst-case bounds when unknown outcomes are unconstrained independently. Verified shared impressions or other constraints may forbid some endpoint assignments; joint enumeration of the allowed assignments then gives sharper bounds. Unknown transcription and unverified die identity must not be treated as known equality constraints.

Uncertain *deposit membership*, *phase* or *exposure* changes denominators and eligible strata. Handle these by an explicit finite set of admissible worlds: assign each object exactly once to one documented possible location, assign disputed phases and evidence labels within their allowed sets, recompute eligibility and Delta, and take the union of bounds. Do not independently minimize denominators or duplicate an uncertain artifact into both groups. A world with no matched stratum is a **test-unavailable world**, not a zero effect and not a world to drop silently. If a conflict has no finite defensible assignment set, report the record separately rather than invent alternatives.

Worst-case bounds over observed unknown texts do not address unreported seal objects, unexcavated deposits or misclassified archaeological evidence. Add separate sensitivity axes: up to k D+/D0 labels wrong; up to m missing seal objects per eligible deposit; and strict versus associated-area exposure. Display how much uncertainty first reverses the sign, with k and m as assumptions rather than measured historical facts. If missing-object counts are unrestricted, an excavation-wide prevalence contrast is not identified. The narrower report-catalogued contrast remains a legitimate, explicitly selected estimand.

## Dependence, duplicate controls and validation

A physical seal appearing in several catalogues counts once. Separate ancient seals sharing a text remain separate objects in the physical-object estimand; deleting them as “duplicates” would change that question. Nevertheless their common text or die can explain apparent replication. Report an alternative one-per-verified-die/inscription-type analysis, distinguish verified from text-similar groups, and show whether the contrast rests on one group. Text types spanning deposits cannot be arbitrarily assigned to whichever group is convenient.

Room 215, passage 286 and courtyard 297 plausibly belong to one linked production complex. Report them individually but treat the complex as one dependence block for any held-out validation or uncertainty claim. Retain separate membership uncertainty when the common episode is only conjectured. Split connected physical-object/die/mold groups together; identical strings alone are a leakage control for a type-generalization analysis, not proof of common production or identity.

A within-stratum permutation test would require defensible exchangeability of the exposure labels among independent complexes, which archaeology and differential reporting may not provide. The present design should therefore lead with finite contrasts, identified sets and leave-one-complex-out sensitivity, not a Fisher test on individual seals. Resampling one complex or several rooms in it cannot generate independent evidence. If a permutation calculation is later included, identify its conditional null and assumptions; it does not automatically test a semantic hypothesis.

The currently published 9/D and 8/E observations, the selected catalogue corrections and the existing site export are discovery data. A blinded provenance reread makes classification less circular, but does not turn familiar inscriptions into a genuine holdout. A meaningful future validation set must be frozen from previously unexamined objects or an independent excavation/sector selected before its joint sign-context result is seen. The available records may support only a descriptive test; that is a useful bounded outcome.
