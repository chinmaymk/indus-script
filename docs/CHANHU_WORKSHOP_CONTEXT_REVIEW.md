# Chanhudaro workshop context review

2026-09-25. Independent bounded archaeological review of Mackay, *Chanhu-daro Excavations 1935–36* (1943). This review does not count signs or assign any reading. Its purpose is to determine the appropriate archaeological comparison unit for testing a proposed association between inscriptions and bead/gemstone work.

## Result

A beadworking association is supported for a specific building and adjoining contexts; the entire excavation square 9/D at every recorded level is not thereby one workshop deposit. Conversely, a comparison that treats every other square as lacking beadwork is contradicted by the excavation report. The suitable observational record is an artifact with its locus, level and reported depositional association, with uncertainty retained. A square-only association can be reported descriptively but does not establish workshop-specific enrichment.

The catalogue already documents a distinction between an artifact’s archaeological existence and survival of a readable inscription. A denominator of all seal objects, all surviving inscribed seals, or unique text types therefore requires explicit definition. No fish counts or readings are made in this review.

## Falsifiable use in a sign–craft test

1. Specify the craft-positive unit using archaeological criteria independent of sign content: unfinished beads, drilling tools, raw material, and explicit floor/locus records. Retain a strict core-room set and a separately reported expanded associated-area set.
2. Assign artifacts to those units from catalogue provenance. Missing loci/levels stay unknown; a square-only location does not get promoted to a room or floor. Keep object count, impression count and unique inscription count distinct.
3. Construct comparison contexts in the same occupational phase with similarly informative provenances. Explicit beadworking contexts outside 9/D are positives or a separate class, never automatic negatives. Absence of reported bead debris is not verified absence of bead production.
4. Estimate association only after fixing these classifications, with sensitivity analyses for uncertain boundaries and levels. A fish-sign association that disappears after including beadworking outside 9/D would weaken a workshop-specific argument. An association surviving these controls would strengthen contextual evidence but still would not choose among commodity, occupation, ownership, administrative and phonetic interpretations by itself.
5. The present review does not claim that no such association exists. It identifies a concrete confound and the additional joins necessary to test it. No phonetic or semantic value follows from any observation here; all 417 sign meanings remain unestablished.
