# Inscribed Harappa jars 5919 and 10252: text bridges

**Both jars are explicitly described as inscribed, but neither jar's own sign sequence has been recovered in this bounded review.** A separate, authenticated bridge leads from a sealing found inside jar 10252 to Vats Plate C, item 671, and Mahadevan reference 4671. That sealing's text must not be assigned to the jar.

| Object | Original evidence | What remains unavailable |
|---|---|---|
| Jar 5919, Table I serial 35 | Vats I p.165; p.253; Table I pp.256–257: a large, inscribed jar, described as crushed in the narrative and napiform in the table | Own sign count, drawing, readable photograph, or authenticated CISI/M77 text reference |
| Jar 10252, Table I serial 116 | Vats I p.253; Table I pp.266–267: round and in pieces; the remarks explicitly report **two lines with seven pictograms** | Those seven characters and an authenticated catalogue or photograph of the jar's inscription |
| Sealing contained in jar 10252 | Vats I p.253 and Table I p.267 explicitly associate the faience sealing with Plate C, item 671; M77 Appendix V p.821 maps EH items 1–713 to references 4001–4713 | Reliable full original reading of its blurred counterpart face; any evidence that this is the jar's own inscription |

The [p.267 crop](../data/raw/inscribed-bridge-vats267-row116.png) shows the distinction directly: the miscellaneous-contents column describes the discoid faience sealing, lentoid in section, while the remarks column describes the jar's two-line inscription. The source reports a contained object, not a seal impression stamped onto this jar.

Jar 5919 also has an unresolved location discrepancy. The [p.165 narrative](../data/raw/inscribed-bridge-vats165-5919.png) places it in square **P23/5**, crushed at 10 feet below the surface. Table I p.256 places serial 35 / register 5919 in **P23/10**, Mound AB, Extension of Pits I and II, stratum IV, also at 10 feet. Both versions are retained. The table's measurement cell is blank. Its associated porous earth, cereal traces, leaves, bone, charcoal and mixed objects do not supply a measured vessel capacity.

Jar 10252's table entry gives Mound F, Trench IV, square I12/4, depth 9 feet and stratum IV. Its measurements are also blank. Cereal, chaff, straw, melon seeds and leaf impressions occur in a mixed deposit with other objects; the review does not establish original-use contents or a commodity reading.

## The contained sealing's bridge

The [M77 appendix rule](../data/raw/inscribed-bridge-m77-EH-rule.png), printed p.821 / PDF830, supplies an explicit source mapping rather than a numerical resemblance. EH item 671 therefore maps to M77 reference 4671. [Vats Plate C](../data/raw/inscribed-bridge-vats-plateC-671.png), PDF208 of the retained plates volume, supplies two labelled views. The lower view is compatible with a curved U-like group and three upright strokes, but it is blurred; the other view is not independently transcribed here.

M77's [printed text entry](../data/raw/inscribed-bridge-m77-4671.png), p.126 / PDF137, has two fields: metadata `210009` with loss hatching, then `20001` with the normalized U-like sign and three strokes. The native export's `+089-328+` is only the surviving sequence; it must not erase the lost counterpart or become the seven-sign jar text. This is a graphical and catalogue identification, not a numerical reading.

The raw Lipi excavation field yields two candidate rows, `212.1` and `212.2`, both labelled `10252d671`, Harappa, faience, `TAB:B`, with strings `+700-033+` and `+000-000-000-000+`. These are consistent source locators for the contained sealing, not jar records. This review has not authenticated the suffix `d` in a primary registration table. Its raw CISI field is blank (`-`), and no global W-to-M mapping or exact lost-text length is inferred. No `5919` excavation-field hit was found.

## Bounded attempts and limits

Exact-number searches of the retained CISI1, CISI2 and M77 PDF text returned no usable bridge. CISI2's native extraction is known to be unhelpful, and its companion OCR also supplies no exact target field-number match. These failures cannot establish that an object is absent from either catalogue; no complete visual CISI catalogue census was performed.

The Vats OCR discussion of inscribed pottery at pp.286–287 points to Plates CII–CIV. Those actual photographic plates were inspected at PDF212, 214 and 216. No explicit 5919/10252 identifier was authenticated on them. A generic two-line or seven-sign resemblance was not accepted as identity evidence. M77's additional Harappa pottery-graffiti entries at Appendix V pp.824–825 likewise supplied no target field-number bridge on the inspected pages.

The [machine-readable review](../data/processed/inscribed_jar_text_bridges.json) records the attempts, exact search outputs, source hashes and limitations. [Crop reproduction](../scripts/render_inscribed_jar_bridge_crops.py) and its [coordinate manifest](../data/processed/inscribed_jar_bridge_crop_manifest.json) preserve unretouched views. All 17 new raw artifacts are listed in the [pending registry](../data/processed/inscribed_jar_bridge_downloads.json), including an independently fetched duplicate of Vats leaf191. That duplicate and the unused blank plate interleaves remain retained, but are not counted as independent evidence.

The remaining requirement is a primary field-number-to-photograph or drawing bridge for each jar itself. The contained sealing establishes a contextual association with jar 10252; it does not provide a readable inscription–contents pair for that jar.
