# M-934 candidate: primary image supports the reversed-comb comparison

Marshall's seal158 is securely linked to excavationDM50 by the original table.
Its old impression has five visible sign groups, compatible with the secondary
M-934 sequence `W526 W555 W235 W002 W861`. The comb is reversed relative to
L-89's comb in the corresponding impression orientation, and the illustrated
face lacks L-9's conspicuous missing comb corner. This materially strengthens
the proposed W526/W527 contextual comparison. It does not establish equal
meaning or sound, and CISI2's own M-934 metadata has not been independently
checked.

## Primary identity rather than parsing a concatenated field

The secondary export's row3329.1 gives `DM50158`. This review did not accept
that string as a field number or split it solely on guesswork. Marshall's
VolumeII printed403 (local PDF54) explicitly gives plate number158, site/serial
**DM50**, 0.95×0.95in, typeB, white steatite, level below surface **4ft7in**.
The original plate's seal number158 then locates the illustrated text.

The secondary dimension and depth fields are compatible with this entry; its
−4.58ft is a decimal rendering close to4ft7in, not an independently measured
precision. The original table supplies no room, house or workshop attribution
for this row. The export's SDStupa label is not newly authenticated here.
DM50, seal158, CISIM-934 and any Mahadevan text number remain distinct identifiers.
No primary Mahadevan appendix bridge was verified in this subtask.

The photograph is in Marshall VolumeIII, Archive item
[`in.gov.ignca.48270`](https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.48270),
[leaf191](../data/raw/m934_marshall48270_leaf191.jpg), the seal plate containing
numbered objects143–191. The scan clips the far-right Roman plate heading;
this review uses exact leaf and seal numbers instead of certifying the cropped
Roman numeral. This is the old impression illustration, not a newly located
original-seal photograph. The [tight crop](../results/primary_pages/m934-mic158.png)
retains the number158.

## Personally inspected observations

In the printed impression, left-to-right spatial order is:

1. A staggered comb-like form with its short transverse bars between the uprights.
2. The crossed/angular form represented by W555 in the export.
3. A fish-shaped form with a cap above, compatible with W235.
4. Two short vertical strokes, compatible with W002.
5. A diamond with an interior mark, compatible with W861.

The comb's staggered uprights have the reverse arrangement to L-89's impression
comb, compatible with the W526 versus W527 distinction. The whole composition
is not simply a horizontally mirrored copy of L-89: the following crossed,
fish, stroke and diamond groups occupy the same sequence positions. L-89 is
a rectangular text-only seal, whereas158 has an animal and stand beneath the
text. The two artifacts are therefore not duplicate views of one physical object.

The seal158 impression is reproduced as a roughly complete square. There is no
obvious missing upper terminal corner of the kind observed on L-9. Surface and
print quality are imperfect, and the small old image does not certify every
incision or physical edge. The assessment is **visible orientation contrast
supported; gross corner-loss explanation not observed**, not pristine condition
or physical autopsy. The capped fish's presence is supported independently of
the comb classification.

The whole-string comparison with L-89 is consequently a useful candidate for
an orientation variant: their secondary strings differ only by W526/W527 and
the primary pictures are consistent with that contrast. Alternative explanations
include distinct signs occupying the same slot. A shared context does not force
allography, identical meaning, or identical pronunciation. Reading direction is
not newly proved; “ending” follows the corpus's reported right-to-left reading.

## Independent M-63 corroboration

As requested, I also inspected the parent's retained CISI1 page28/PDF64,
`pincer-m63-64.png`. M-63's original and impression show a staggered comb
whose arrangement is reversed relative to L-89's corresponding impression.
Both views agree after reflection; no major missing comb-bearing corner is
apparent. This independently supports an intentional orientation difference
being present somewhere in the corpus, rather than all W526 occurrences being
explained by L-9's break. The two crossed forms in M-63 are visibly different,
but the parent is responsible for their full catalogue bridge. No semantic
inference or frequency claim follows from this visual corroboration.

## Reproduction, provenance and failed attempts

All new downloads are in `data/raw/`, with exact URLs, sizes and SHA256 in
[`m934_new_sources.json`](../data/processed/m934_new_sources.json). The download
script records sources as each succeeds. Primary plate coordinates are in
[`render_m934_crops.py`](../scripts/render_m934_crops.py); no enhancement,
reconstruction or reflection was applied to the crops.

  source after its existing registry identified a1982–83 interim report.
- Web searches did not locate a usable Archive volume directly. The retained
  Archive advanced-search response identified item48270 as VolumeIII.

  confidence is0 and blank numbers did not supply a reliable plate map.
- Leaf203 was a wrong navigation guess: it is plateCXVI, not seal158.
- Leaf183 contains seal numbers19–40, not158. Leaf189 contains105–142.
  Both were retained. Leaf191 contains the actual158 illustration.
- The OCR XML was downloaded to resolve irregular/misleading plate navigation;
  cropped/unclear Roman headings are not treated as authoritative IDs.
- OCR searches for a primary Mahadevan appendix bridge did not establish one;
  no such bridge is claimed.

No semantic or phonetic value has been assigned, no original source was edited,
and no central source registry was modified by this subtask.
