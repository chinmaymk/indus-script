# Inscribed jars and cereal remains: what the original table establishes

**Three jars are explicitly reported as inscribed, but their own sign sequences
remain unidentified.** A separate inscribed faience object can be traced to a
jar containing cereal and other remains. That contextual link is real; it does
not show that its signs mean grain. Screening the complete right-hand table
pages reveals both common cereal reports and missing inscription-status data.

## The contained object's authenticated text bridge

For10252 the source explicitly distinguishes a discoid faience sealing among
the contents from the seven pictograms on the jar. The contained object is
Vats PlateC, item671. M77's explicit AppendixV rule maps EH671 to reference4671.
The published M77 entry has a lost counterpart and a surviving normalized field
represented in the native export as `+089-328+`. The photo is compatible with a
U-like group and three strokes, but does not independently recover the lost face.
It is not a seven-sign jar text. The
[bridge review](INSCRIBED_JAR_TEXT_BRIDGES.md) preserves the primary pages,
photographs, source mapping and their limits.

This adds a **contained-object association** for the surviving M089/M328 field.
It cannot distinguish a commodity label from ownership, amount, office, ritual
use or a reused object deposited with the jar. These alternatives are possible
explanations, not established translations. The source does not describe this
object making a seal impression on the jar, and no sealing function is inferred
from a catalogue noun alone. No global W-to-M conversion was added.

## Why the data do not identify an association

Among the three positively identified inscribed jars, two have cereal-related
reports and one has no such report. The other173 jars have **unestablished
inscription status**, not observed absence of writing. Even if cereal mention
were treated as a perfectly observed outcome, the missing inscription statuses
can produce opposite associations:

| Allowed completion of missing inscription status | Difference in cereal-report rate, inscribed minus other |
|---|---:|
| Put every cereal-report row in the inscribed group, retaining the known inscribed nonmention | +60/61, about+98.36 percentage points |
| Put every nonmention row in that group, retaining the two known inscribed cereal reports | −58/59, about−98.31 percentage points |

These are exact mathematical bounds on a **documentary reporting outcome**,
not estimates of ancient behaviour. The script enumerates6,843 possible grouped
completions. A separate [closed-form verification](../scripts/verify_inscribed_jar_bounds.py)
proves both extremes are achievable and no completion exceeds them. Excluding
the two qualified cereal descriptions still allows both directions. Actual
grain absence and inscription timing are additionally unknown, so archaeological
claims would require more evidence still. No association p-value is warranted.

## Preserved result and next evidential requirement

All original responses and duplicate acquisitions remain in `data/raw/`; hashes
and types distinguish downloaded images, headers and local derivatives. Two
parallel requests duplicated existing page images before coordination arrived;
those identical bodies count as one source witness, not replication. The
[research log](RESEARCH_LOG.md) records failed page locators, corrections and
catalogue searches as well as successful joins.

The advance is a securely typed text-to-context link plus an independently
checked comparison frame. To test a grain meaning, the jars' own photographed
texts and a comparison set with independently observed inscription status are
still needed. To test quantities, capacities would also need measurement.
All417 semantic and phonetic assignments remain null; this investigation does
not complete the decipherment goal.
