# Pettersson's orientation notation: attribution correction

**The DK7857 drawing is compatible with Pettersson's `7i 4t` notation.** Our initial inference that its upright strokes refuted a later transverse-stroke assignment misrepresented his definition. Pettersson uses `t` for strokes arranged in a tilted **row**, not specifically for individually horizontal strokes. This correction establishes fair attribution; it does not establish the numerical values of `i` or `t`, or prove that every physical source agrees.

In the original [1999 paper](https://insa.nic.in/writereaddata/UpLoadedFiles/IJHS/Vol34_2_1_JSPettersson.pdf), the terminology on printed p.89 (PDF1) identifies `t` with strokes in tilted rows. Page90 explains that the row lies at an angle to other rows, and that this extra notation preserves information lost in sequential standardized transcriptions. The upright four-mark row tilted above the seven-mark row in Mackay CXXVII1 is compatible with that description.

The reviewed definition, object table, discussion and conclusion do **not** explicitly assign individually horizontal strokes to DK7857. The bounded finding concerns those reviewed passages, not an assertion about every publication or physical depiction. We inspected pp.89–92,97–102,105–108; the fresh renders are pp.89–90, while the remaining page images were already retained.

The valid distinctions are between stroke orientation, row orientation, normalized sign identity, and hypothetical numerical value. Mackay's prose for DK7800 specifically describes a vertical/horizontal contrast; that separate observation must not be silently transferred to DK7857. Nor does Pettersson's willingness to test `t=i` mean he ultimately endorsed equal values: his conclusion argues against conflating them. Testing an alternative and preferring another interpretation are compatible parts of his paper.
