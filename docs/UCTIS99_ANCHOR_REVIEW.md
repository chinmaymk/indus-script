# UCTIS 099: external-name anchor audit

UCTIS 099 is **BDTNS 205179, Iraq Museum IM 226931**, a Sumerian administrative tablet attributed to Irisaĝrig and dated by BDTNS to **Šū-Suen 09-01-00**. The public record has an explicit **Seal: N** flag. Its text identifies oil recipients associated with Meluhha; it supplies no deciphered Indus inscription or bilingual sign alignment. Review date: 2026-09-23.

## Citation and publication identity

Surdi and De Graef (2025), note 22, list UCTIS 099 alongside CUSAS 40-2 1354 and 1582, and Nisaba 15/2 0371. The note groups names from those references and is not a four-tablet concordance. [Article, note 22](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00856401.2025.2488137).

The abbreviation was authenticated using BDTNS's own bibliography search, not expanded by analogy: **Basima Jalil Abid (Bāsma Yālil 'Abdu), _Unpublished cuneiform texts of the Third Dynasty of Ur from the the city of Iri-Sag-rig7 / Al-Šarraki_, 2017 [in Arabic]**. The duplicated “the” is in the catalogue title. The result identifies 97 edited texts and links ResearchGate publication 350889492; the full original publication was not obtained. Its publisher, original page/plate number and precise publication format remain unresolved. [Abbreviation result](https://bdtns.cesga.es/resultado_bibliografia.php?abreviatura=UCTIS); [record bibliography](https://bdtns.cesga.es/resultado_bibliografia.php?id_bdts=205179).

The catalogue query for `UCTIS 099` returned one record, IM 226931. Its stable public catalogue is [BDTNS 205179](https://bdtns.cesga.es/205179). The header's CDLI-equivalence slot is blank and no CDLI export link was exposed. **No P-number is assigned in this audit.** This is a bounded failure to establish a CDLI cross-reference, not evidence that none exists.

## Text, people and evidence limits

BDTNS credits the transliteration to Abid (2017), with revisions by M. Molina (2020) and J. Arrojería (2022). The following is a manual rendering of the current catalogue text; superscripts/subscripts are flattened, while restoration and editorial addition markers are retained:

| Locator | Catalogue reading |
|---|---|
| Obverse 1 | `1 sila3 i3-giš` |
| Obverse 2 | `Na-na-za` |
| Obverse 3 | `1 sila3 Sa6-ma-ar` |
| Obverse 4 | `½ sila3 A-li-a-hi dam-a-ni` |
| Obverse 5 | `[i3]-ba lu2 Me-luh-<ha>ki-me` |
| Reverse 1 | `zi-ga iti šu-gar` |
| Reverse 2 | `mu dŠu-dSuen [lugal] Uri5ki-ma-ke4 e2 dŠara2 Ummaki-ka mu-du3` |

These are the one-, one-, and half-sila oil allocations to Nanaza, Samar and Ali-ahi, with `dam-a-ni` identifying Ali-ahi as his wife in the adjacent Samar entry. The Meluhha designation contains editorially supplied `<ha>` and restored `[i3]`; these must not be silently presented as fully preserved signs. No merchant, interpreter or shepherd title appears in this short UCTIS text. The herdsmen description in P453801/P516366 must not be imported as wording of UCTIS 099. Names attached to rations do not determine a seal owner.

The linked [Abid handcopy](https://bdtns.cesga.es/UR3_imagenes/imagenes/imagenes_076/U205179_2.jpg) was downloaded and visually inspected. It shows two drawn cuneiform faces and no separate seal depiction or legend. It is a handcopy, not a photograph, and this review does not independently collate each cuneiform sign. The explicit database `N` and the drawing jointly provide no positive seal anchor; they do not substitute for examining every physical surface.

## Comparison and deduplication

P453801 and P516366 have the same oil amounts and the same three-name pattern. Current CDLI reads `na-na-sa3`, whereas UCTIS's current BDTNS text reads `Na-na-za`; the earlier identity audit preserves those version differences. Their reverse formula includes royal offerings and herdsmen, a different month and year name, and an illegible seal entry. UCTIS instead has the Šara temple-building year and month `šu-gar`, catalogue date SS09-01-00 and accession IM 226931. These differences support a separate **catalogued text/event** and do not justify merging UCTIS with those records merely because its obverse formula repeats. They also do not prove physical independence without comparing objects and resolving metadata errors. P453801/P516366 retain their own explicitly reported possible-duplicate relationship. See [identity audit](P453801_IDENTITY.md).

The downloaded [P516138 / CUSAS 40, 1354](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/516138) JSON and ATF were also inspected. They repeat the same recipient pattern and ration amounts, with damage in the first allocation, and include the royal-offering/herdsmen clauses. The month is `iti {gesz}apin`; catalogue `dates_referenced` is Šū-Suen.06.03.00, while the ATF's year formula is damaged. Its seal has three lines `[...] / dub-[sar] / dumu [...]`, with no surviving personal name in that transcription. These differences from UCTIS's SS09-01 date, short reverse and Seal=N flag provide no positive basis for merging the records. Both refer to the same name pattern, but neither establishes an Indus spelling of those names. A complete physical-duplicate exclusion remains beyond this catalogue comparison.

## Consequence and falsifiable follow-up

UCTIS 099 strengthens the cuneiform evidence for this named group and its Meluhha designation. It supplies **zero authenticated Indus name spellings** in this audit. A useful next test is a physical accession and image comparison against the other three citations, retaining date and damaged-sign differences. A phonetic anchor would additionally require an actual Indus inscription demonstrably associated with a specific named person and a defensible assignment of the name to that inscription; this record does not provide that bridge.

All downloaded source files, including the search forms and unused navigation frame, are listed with exact URLs, sizes and SHA-256 in `data/processed/uctis99_anchor_review.json`. The failed Borrelli PDF download returned HTTP 403 and saved no PDF. Search-engine failure and the missing CDLI U-list entry were discovery limits, not negative evidence about the tablet's existence. No sign reading or universal absence claim follows from this bounded audit.
