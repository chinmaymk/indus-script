# Umma reproduced image: independent visual review

28 September 2026. **Panel D is now available and visually inspected.** This is an additive update to the frozen `UMMA_ANCHOR_AUDIT.md`, whose earlier image-retrieval failure remains an accurate record of that attempt. The new observation does not authenticate a field identifier, excavation context or reading.

The retained figure `data/raw/umma-anchor-renewed-figure.webp` is 1000 × 924 pixels. Panel D occupies only about 150 × 140 pixels of it. Its small native resolution limits the strokes and surface details that can be distinguished. The saved 162 × 151 crop includes the whole visible panel with a small white margin; it is not enlarged, mirrored, rotated, sharpened or restored.

## What is actually visible

Panel D presents a roughly rectangular grey surface, with comparatively straight upper and right boundaries and an irregular, rough lower boundary. The lower-left area is also irregular. Dark, discontinuous lines cross the upper-left surface; at this resolution they can be described as crack-like breaks, but their depth, age and relation to any damage cannot be established. Much of the lower field is rough or visually unclear. The image does not justify reconstructing a complete original outline.

In the upper band, two forms are substantially clearer than the remaining traces. Near the middle is a vertically elongated closed oval, with tapered ends, enclosing a small vertical/branched structure. To its right is a tall U-like form with short outward horizontal projections near its upper ends. Other short or angular traces toward the left are intersected by the rough/cracked region and cannot be confidently segmented or counted. These are geometric descriptions, not sign-number assignments.

Below the upper band is an animal-like figure. Its head is toward the **left as displayed**, with a long body extending to the right; horn-like projections and slanting short strokes in the neck/shoulder area are discernible. Lower outlines, legs and the far-right part are insufficiently clear for a complete anatomical description. No animal species or diagnostic iconographic identity is asserted.

The panel supplies one displayed face/view only. Its photographic relief is insufficient to independently decide whether this is a seal surface, an impression, a cast, or an image whose contrast has been prepared in publication. Therefore display orientation is not script direction, and no mirrored or 180-degree version is substituted. No reverse, boss, perforation, scale bar or field-number label is visible in panel D.

## Attribution is distinct from object authentication

The retained Harappa caption identifies panels D and E as Umma-related items and attributes **D** to the SAHI report, p59, Fig6.61, with credit to the Slovak Archaeological and Historical Institute. It credits **E** separately to the Ashmolean Museum. These are distinct panels and attributions; panel E must not be used as a second view of D. [Reproduced figure and caption](https://www.harappa.com/content/indus-valley-early-commercial-connections-central-and-western-asia).

This verifies the reproduced-image-to-caption relationship. It does **not** independently inspect the cited primary report page. The exact label **TJ17-PRO-S56** remains unverified by this image/caption, and no field-number join is made from a discovery post. Likewise, neither a sealed-goods context, excavation rather than survey recovery, material identification nor a link to a cuneiform document follows from this small reproduced image.
