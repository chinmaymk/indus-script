# GlossaLab: selected sign identities do not match Mahadevan's forms

**The five selected anchor descriptions do not provide reliable pictorial
identifications in the stated Mahadevan namespace.** This conclusion concerns
which sign is pictured and the stated basis for an assignment. It is not a
shape-based disproof of every possible conventional meaning or sound.

The proposal is the locally archived GlossaLab snapshot identified as commit
`40812fc54470d5508b47af5fabcea7ccf044036c`. I inspected the supplied numbered glyph
images and Mahadevan1977 TableI page renders, rather than using another
proposal's verbal labels to identify the forms. The
[evidence ledger](../data/processed/glossa_sign_identity_review.json) records
source hashes, input JSON pointers, exact row locators and limits.

| ID | Proposal's stated identification or basis | Form observed in both local glyph and primary table | Primary locator |
|---|---|---|---|
| M012 | Single stroke, numeral one | Branched anthropomorphic centre, crossbar and paired lateral loops | Printed717/PDF728, left row12 |
| M047 | Plain fish | Bent/forked stem ending in a small loop or leaf-like enclosure | Printed717/PDF728, right row15 |
| M059 | Numeral seven | Plain fish-like pointed oval, appendages and forked tail; not seven parallel strokes | Printed717/PDF728, right row27 |
| M099 | Jar/vessel | Two separate short parallel bars | Printed718/PDF729, right row3 |
| M328 | Man/person | Simple open-topped U, without a drawn head or limbs | Printed722/PDF733, left row8 |

The source crosswalk additionally describes M099 as bow/archer, inconsistent
with the anchor's vessel description and with the observed two-bar form.
M059 supplies the plain fish-like morphology that the proposal attributes to
M047. This does not automatically transfer the proposal's sound or meaning to
M059: identifying a fish-shaped glyph is not identifying its ancient reading.

In particular, the assignment of seven to M059 is not logically refuted by
the outline alone. The source says “Numeral 7,” not “seven strokes.” A non-tally
sign could conventionally mean seven; the visible form supplies no direct tally
basis for that value. The same distinction applies to a U-shaped glyph hypothetically
denoting a person or two bars hypothetically denoting a vessel.

## Numbering check

The relevant TableI rows do not print individual numeric sign IDs. Their
identification uses sequential table order: PDF728 has M001–M032 on the left
and M033–M064 on the right; PDF729 continues with M065–M096 and M097–M128;
PDF733 has M321–M352 and M353–M384. The indicated row shapes were checked
against the numbered supplied images. Existing table transcription supplied
locators, not a substitute for viewing those rows.

The proposal also maps **W001 to M001**. The independent local feature catalogue's
P121 entry instead links W001 to the short-stroke forms M097/M098. Direct inspection
confirms that M001 is a stick-person-like form, while M097/M098 are isolated
short strokes. This is a substantive mapping conflict, not zero-padding.
No primary Wells plate was independently authenticated in this task, so the
Wells side remains explicitly attributed to that separate catalogue source.

## Consequence for testing

The pictorial support offered for these selected anchors cannot be carried
into a decipherment test unchanged. Sign identities and crosswalks need to be
re-established before recalculating analyses that depend on them. Correcting a
label would not by itself validate its associated phonetic value, confidence
level or language assignment.

This is a targeted five-sign review, not an estimate of the proposal's overall
error rate. No source values were silently changed and no phonetic reading was
assigned.
