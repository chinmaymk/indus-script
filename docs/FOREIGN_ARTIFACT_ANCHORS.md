# Foreign artifact anchors: exact joins and semantic limits

The bounded review and its follow-up establish three exact M77-to-museum joins and a fourth M77-to-primary excavation report. They also record two Gulf comparison cases whose inscriptions are questioned. None supplies an independently known word, quantity, personal name or translation aligned to an Indus sequence.

M77's 8000-series belongs to Kalibangan. Its West Asian list is the 9800-series; the 9900-series is explicitly only probable West Asia (Appendix V, printed829/PDF838). Equal numbers in different publications are not crosswalks.

## Susa: an exact bibliographic bridge

M77 9801 cites Delaporte's catalogue, volumeI, plateXXV fig15. The Louvre assigns that exact publication entry to **SB2425 / AS41 / CCO S299**. This is a cylinder seal of steatite,23mm high and16mm in diameter, described with two bovines and an Indus inscription. Its form differs from the familiar square stamp seal, but neither the cylinder form nor the animals identifies the inscription's meaning. The museum's2400–2100BCE dating and Indus-origin attribution are catalogue assessments, not a stratigraphic context independently recovered here. [Primary Louvre record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010176471).

Do not confuse it with the circular Susa stamp **SB5614 / AS15374**, a different artifact. The SB2425 JSON record includes two official photograph URLs, preserved in the ledger. The new photographs were not inspected at glyph level in this audit.

## Ur: secure object identity, limited burial association

M77 9846 cites Gadd1932 no16. The official British Museum entry for **123208 /1932,1008.178** cites that same number and reports a circular, engraved steatite seal with a bull and a possibly pseudo-Indus inscription. Its diameter is22mm; one side is badly chipped. It came from the fill of the shaft of PG1847, not an independently identified named individual's sealed container. A burial context therefore cannot convert its signs into a personal name or funerary formula. [Primary museum record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1932-1008-178).

The entry also cites **Mitchell1986 no2**. That is not **Gadd1932 no2**, which is M77 9832. The [subsequent primary-source audit](BM122187_AUDIT.md) establishes9832 as BM122187. Gadd explicitly says its precise findspot is unknown. Do not transfer123208 or PG1847 to9832; candidate registration1929,1017.725 remains unconfirmed.

## Kish: association is neither bilingualism nor secure early dating

M77 9822 points to Langdon1931 p595 figB. The publisher's primary extract reports a seal found9m below the surface at Kish but warns that its associated stone pommel bore an inscription not earlier than Sargon; both objects might have fallen from higher deposits. The deep find is therefore not, on this evidence, a secure pre-Sargonic date. The readable inscription belongs to another object: no source here establishes that it translates the seal, names its owner or repeats its content. A museum accession and the original figure were not independently authenticated. [Langdon's primary report](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0035869X00110615).

## Gulf comparisons: separate script membership from foreign provenance

Laursen's newly reported no11, Fig1b, is linked to Karzakkan burial mound **BBM14569** in Table1. He describes a recognizable initial twin-like form followed by imitation strokes. This is a useful independently recorded burial context, but the claim that every mark must be an ordinary Indus sign would prejudge precisely what is disputed.

The same paper identifies its Failaka no13 with **Kjærum1983 cat279** and characterizes its unusual inscription and later Dilmun style as possible emulation. This is a bibliographic lead, not an independently inspected original excavation catalogue. Neither case is assigned a new M77 number or forced into the417-sign inventory. [Laursen2010, pp98–100 and122–123](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0471.2010.00329.x).

The report's Janabiyah no10 is a further lead with a worn sequence and no BBM number in Table1; it is not silently merged with no11. Geographic provenance alone does not establish a different language, nor does anomalous spelling establish meaningless imitation.

## What these cases can test

The joins support tests that stratify objects by independently recorded find site, object form, damage and inscription-status uncertainty. They cannot train translations using animal pictures, burial labels or nearby cuneiform objects as if these were bilingual equivalents. A statistical departure from domestic sequences would remain compatible with foreign language use, local variants, copying errors, imitation, selection and preservation differences.

As a separate exclusion control, BM120573/U7683 is described by its museum as an Indus-style seal with a **Sumerian** inscription, found on the surface at Diqdiqqah. Its iconography does not make its writing an Indus bilingual. It is not substituted for the numbered Indus cases above.
