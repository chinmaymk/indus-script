# M-897 CISI2: independent visual assessment

Date: 2026-09-27. **The new original-seal and impression photographs improve the visible evidence, but do not securely settle the fine W550/W555 distinction. Physical fragmentation is now supported by the original object's outline and truncated animal motif, rather than only by the old FEM plate cutout.** This is one artifact shown in two views, not two independent sign witnesses. No semantic or phonetic value is assigned.

## Images and identity inspected

CISI2 printed85/PDF120 labels the photographs M-897 A and M-897 a. The capital/lowercase distinction is the corpus convention for the original face and its impression. Both views and the full page were personally inspected, using the parent's unretouched crops. Their layouts are naturally reversed; no extra horizontal flip was applied in this assessment.

Printed436/PDF471 independently gives M-897, Finnish concordance2545, excavationDK9111, museumMD446, and photographyJL589 with impressionP368. This supports the modern catalogue alias and field identity. Finnish2545 is not being equated to a Mahadevan text reference. The earlier original Mackay545↔DK9111 bridge is documented in `M897_PRIMARY_REVIEW.md`; this assessment does not alter that older report.

## Strokes before code classification

In the published impression, the target is the second broad group from the left, between the comb and capped fish. In the original it is the corresponding second group from the right. The two views show intersecting oblique strokes, wedge-shaped or triangular intervening spaces, and bent terminal projections. The group is more complex than a plain isolated X. Portions of the lower oblique strokes and their bends are clearer here than in the old FEM impression.

The upper part is close to a rough, uneven top boundary. Some tips merge with the surrounding rough surface or with strong light and shadow; their exact endpoints are not consistently distinct in both views. The photographs are reproductions from the retained scan, and larger display cannot recover missing detail. The nearby comb and the central capped fish remain recognizable. The image order is a spatial description, not a new claim about ancient reading direction.

Comparison used the original and impression of M-63, the L-89 pair, and Mahadevan's numbered sign-list forms216 and222 at printed33–34/PDF44–45. Those comparisons show why the bent ends and their joins matter. They do not remove the uncertainty at the corresponding tips on M-897. The M-897 group belongs broadly with the crossed triangular forms under investigation, but **this reviewer cannot securely choose W550 versus W555 from these views**. Neither an intact distinguishing terminal nor a specific break demonstrably removing it is certified.

Thus the raw W550 transcription should remain attributed to its secondary source. A contextual preference for M222/W555 must not be used to invent the faint terminal geometry. Conversely, uncertainty is not positive evidence that the secondary W550 is erroneous. No recoding or global equivalence is proposed.

## Physical condition and evidence unit

The original A view itself has a visibly irregular outer contour and an incomplete animal motif cut by the lower object boundary. The impression preserves corresponding truncation in reversed orientation. This gives direct support for a physically fragmentary seal, beyond the former uncertainty about cropping of the old plate image. The new photographs do not show the full reverse or a fracture cross-section, and they do not establish the date or cause of breakage.

The broad missing lower part is not a demonstrated loss of a particular crossed-sign terminal. Roughness near the upper inscription and the lower fragment boundary are separate observations. It would be unsound to infer that all text is complete because five broad groups are visible, or that a diagnostic W555 stroke must once have existed because the seal is fragmentary.

The original and its impression are **one artifact, two complementary views**. They help check mirrored placement and distinguish relief from illumination, but do not increase the corpus sample size to two. M-63 and L-89 are comparison objects, not identity substitutes.
