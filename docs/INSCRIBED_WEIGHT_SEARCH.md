# Search for inscribed Harappan stone weights

**No qualifying same-object mass-and-inscription anchor was found in this bounded review.** The examined excavation evidence provides measured weights, but does not authenticate Indus marks on those same objects. This is a scoped negative result, not a claim that every surviving weight or later excavation has been checked.

The criterion was a secure object identity, a physically measured mass, archaeological attribution, and demonstrably ancient marks on that exact object. A seal found nearby, a modern catalogue number painted on a weight, natural stone banding, or a proposed unit inferred from mass alone does not meet it.

## Excavation evidence

Hemmy's chapter in Marshall, *Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization* II (1931), states on printed p.596: “No inscription nor mark of value has been found on any of the weights.” The statement also appears on p.589. The p.596 scan was visually checked. This directly limits using that measured collection as an inscription anchor. Hemmy's ensuing suggestion that the traders were illiterate does **not** follow from the absence of weight labels and is not adopted here. [Primary chapter scan](https://rws.xoba.com/indus_weights/Marshall-1931-Weights-Mohenjodaro.pdf).

Vats, *Excavations at Harappa* I (1940), pp.360–364, describes the weight classes and measured examples. Original pp.360–361 and volume II plate CXVIII were visually checked; the remaining section was inspected through the existing OCR. Three concrete examples from p.361 illustrate the missing inscription link:

|---|---:|---|---|
|635|2652.8 g|CXVIII, 4 and 13: two views of one object|Mound F, Trench I, M12/17; 4 ft below surface; Stratum II|

No ancient inscription is authenticated by these entries or depicted views. Plate photographs contain modern catalogue labels and stone patterning; these must not become sign transcriptions. Not every face is illustrated. Object 635 is chipped, and its reported mass is not a reconstructed intact value. The original corrects OCR errors: **2652.8**, not 2052.8; **4368**, not 4363. Vats also cautions that the plate cutouts are not proportional in size. [Original p.361](https://archive.org/download/in.gov.ignca.18220/page/n389.jpg), [plates volume](https://archive.org/download/in.gov.ignca.9842/9842.pdf).

## A promising but inapplicable stroke-mark lead

Hemmy's appendix in Mackay, *Further Excavations at Mohenjo-daro* I (1938), printed p.675, discusses weights of **2.30 and 2.25 g with four strokes**, and **540 g with six strokes**. These are **Susa** examples in a disputed proposed “Indus type” group. Hemmy challenges their assignment to the Indus system; the heading is not an authentication of Harappan origin. The excerpt supplies neither exact artifact IDs nor drawings of the strokes. Consequently these observations cannot assign readings to M77 signs or establish an Indus unit. The complete p.675 image was checked. [Primary chapter and appendix scan](https://www.rws.xoba.com/indus_weights/Hemmy-1938-weights-Mohenjodaro-Mackay.pdf).

A museum search also surfaced BM134301 / 1963,0715.1, a roughly 16 kg cuneiform-inscribed duck weight displayed under a Harappan trade theme. The museum page explicitly places it in Mesopotamia. Exhibition grouping is not archaeological provenance. It was excluded without assigning any Indus identity. [Museum record](https://networksofthepast.csmvs.in/object/duck-weight-with-cuneiform-inscription/).

## Preservation and limits

The structured review is `data/processed/weight_anchor_structure_review.json`. The four new public-source downloads total 6,323,722 bytes, all under `data/raw/weight_anchor_structure_*`; exact URLs, sizes and SHA-256 hashes are in `data/processed/weight_anchor_structure_downloads.json`. The existing Vats plates/OCR were reused. Primary-page render hashes are in the review ledger. No central source registry was edited.

This review covered the named excavation chapters and two targeted web-search batches; the parent separately reviews Kenoyer 2010. A useful next lead must be a concrete published exception: an exact museum/excavation ID with calibrated mass and all-face images demonstrating ancient marks. Until then, measured weight regularities and independently documented writing remain separate evidence. No numerical sign value, spoken reading or commodity meaning is established here.
