# Chanhudaro deposit exposure review

28 September 2026. This is a finite source-exposed archaeological review of Mackay's architectural narrative through printed page 74, made without inspecting inscription strings or sign-frequency results. It develops the earlier workshop review; it is not a blind holdout. All six new downloaded page images and their response headers are in `data/raw`; the separate pending manifest is `data/processed/chanhu_deposit_new_sources.json`.

The record supports bead production beyond the previously exposed room-215 complex. It also supports two useful comparison candidates: the drainage/bath vicinity at locus 442 and the metalworking hoard at locus 284. These are meaningful reporting categories, not demonstrated absence of beadworking. Their recorded recovery and deposit associations differ from the best bead-production floor. The exposure review alone does not establish a matched artifact-to-deposit comparison.

## Finite screen and evidential scope

The retained OCR, `data/raw/c40_mackay1943_djvu.txt`, was screened within lines 820–5576 for production terms, unfinished material, drills, hones, raw stones and related craft evidence, with surrounding architectural paragraphs read. This includes the narrative through page 64 and the opening pottery discussion through page 74. It is a finite source screen, not an exhaustive inventory of every excavated object. Search hits are locators. Substantive new context assignments below were checked against the page images; three lesser OCR-only leads are explicitly deferred in the JSON.

The JSON supplies 21 context records with locus IDs, square attribution, phase provenance, elevations, association precision, rival crafts, named seal IDs where the same passage provides them, exact OCR line ranges and image paths. Overlapping records are not 21 independent deposits. In particular, the aggregate footnotes overlap the individual loci. No numerical elevation is converted mechanically into an occupation phase.

## Comparison candidates and rival crafts

**Locus 442, square 9/B (p40)** is the strongest additional lower-evidence reporting context linked in the narrative to named seals. A 44-inch-square patch of flooring slopes to a drain; footnote 10 supplies +6.7 ft for this feature. Pan 3209, possibly a bath, was at the same level as the floor. Seals **3215 and 3207** were from the same vicinity and near the same level. This is more informative than a bare findspot or silence: architecture, drainage, a vessel and actual recovered seals are described. Nevertheless, the seals are not said to lie directly on that floor, no exact seal level is supplied here, and the recovered assemblage is not established as an exhaustive floor sample. The prior review's catalogue assignment of 3215 to locus 283 at +6.9 ft must remain visible; this review does not resolve the catalogue/narrative bridge by rounding levels. The parent is independently reviewing the full catalogue. Classify 442 as a candidate comparison reporting context, not “beadworking absent.”

**Locus 284, square 8/B (p40)** contains hoard 2365a–q, explicitly buried at **+9.5 ft**, including several unfinished metal castings and illustrated in situ in Plate LXXV.1. This is a bounded alternative craft assemblage. The surviving room floor was +8.6 ft; do not call the hoard a floor deposit at that level. The house had largely disappeared at the mound edge. Nearby seal **2390** was at **locus 235, +9.9 ft**, and is not stated to be inside hoard 284. The hoard is useful archaeological comparison evidence, but selected hoard recovery differs from a bead scatter on a floor. Beadworking elsewhere in the house is unknown.

Other rival craft records are less suitable as independent comparisons:

- **213, 9/C:** metal hoard 2360a–g buried at +10.4 ft (p41), close to bead-positive court 190; do not make it a clean independent bead-negative household.
- **Near 212:** metal group 2593 including a casting, about one foot below pavement 212 (p41). The pavement's +10.2 ft does not make approximately +9.2 an explicitly recorded hoard elevation.
- **Near 181, 9/E:** unfinished shell bangles 2871 near a privy/drain, approximately at the same level (p48). The drain is +8.2 ft and pavement ten inches higher; the bangle passage does not securely distinguish which height it references.
- **172, 9/E:** unfinished stone weight 2380 at +9.4 ft (p48). Stone-weight work is related lapidary evidence, not automatically bead production.
- **Near 121, 7/E:** unfinished seals **3198, 3052, 3159** and drill-cap 4537 in the close vicinity of a privy (p50). Architecture has stated elevations, but these are not explicitly the unfinished seals' find levels. Drill caps do not uniquely identify beadmaking.
- **Near 171, 9/E:** paste plaques and cylinders in ash/dark soil at +6.8 ft, footnote 39 (p48). This is a distinct potential firing/manufacturing assemblage; interpreting it as bead production requires further evidence.

The p52 synthesis notes unfinished metal objects and hoards but no recovered smelting/casting furnaces; Mackay suggests metal may have been cast elsewhere and brought in for finishing. Absence of a furnace is not absence of metalworking. Equally, columns interpreted as open workshops (p49) do not identify a particular craft without associated material.

## Exclusions, corrections and research log

Finished decorated carnelian beads, including 2797 at 125/+9.7 ft and 3999 at 473/+3.2 ft, are not production evidence on their own. Rooms described as empty or having few objects do not supply informative bead-negative exposure merely because the publication omits bead debris. Harappa III rooms and the briefly excavated Mound I material do not automatically provide phase-matched Harappa II controls.

Three OCR-only leads remain unpromoted: the beadmaking block near 223 on p28, a bead-hone near 121 in the upper/mixed context around pp33–34, and block 3634 near 130 on p45. They are recorded as failed/incomplete locators, not silently discarded or assigned production deposits. Their existence prevents claiming that the image-verified ledger is the entire site's craft inventory.
