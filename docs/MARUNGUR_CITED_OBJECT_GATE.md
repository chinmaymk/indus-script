## Source observations

The announcement attributes examination to K. Rajan, Y. Subbarayalu and
V. Vedachalam. Its second sherd has two Tamil-Brahmi letters read as a-m,
followed by two unread graffiti described by Rajan as resembling Indus signs.
The front of that sherd is missing. The caption identifies this second sherd
in the photograph and credits Rajan. The four forms and their order correspond
to the candidate illustration: a photo-level join, without a field/accession ID.

The specialists tentatively date the inscriptions palaeographically to the
first century BCE. That differs from the candidate table's 200 BCE. The report
describes earthmoving damage to three urns and collection from separate urns.
It suggests personal names and possible deceased owners; neither is a known
referent independently establishing the candidate's proposed meaning.

## Consequences and stopping rule

The published a-m agrees with the candidate's initial letters. Counting this as
validation of the unread completion would count information already known from
Tamil-Brahmi as evidence for the proposed Indus values. Conversely, unread
graffiti are not an independently contradictory reading. No accuracy score or
binary full-reading verdict is justified by this partial agreement.

Rescue circumstances supply no demonstrated simultaneous burial or common
original use. Tentative palaeography supplies no absolute date for each incision.
Mixed marks on an identified sherd establish coexistence; they do not establish
aligned bilingual content, the sound of the unread marks, or their descent from
Indus signs. The direction of the mixed sequence is not independently proved.

The finite object/readings/context gate is closed. The source was already cited
by the candidate and is not a withheld test. No fitting, retranscription, corpus
amendment, dictionary run or experiment rerun occurred. Independent criticism
agrees with the distinction between partial agreement and complete validation.
Reopen only for a specifically identified independent reading, aligned known
content, or new artifact-level evidence assigning values to the unread marks.
