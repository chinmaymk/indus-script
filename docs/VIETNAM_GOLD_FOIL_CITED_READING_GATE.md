# Vietnam gold foil: provisional identity and an earlier tentative reading

11 October 2026. The cited chapter already proposes a tentative Varaha reading
for a plausible matching foil. The candidate's simplified illustration does not
securely identify that object. Consequently this check supplies neither a secure
full-reading validation nor an unknown-Indus-sign-to-sound correspondence.

## Fixed target and acquired source

The target was fixed before acquisition: candidate Table 11 row 4's illustrated
Vietnam gold foil, claimed 100 CE, varāha and Varaha avatar. The frozen protocol
is `data/processed/vietnam_gold_foil_cited_reading_gate_protocol_v1.json`.

## Reading, identity and chronology

Entry No. 08, **93-GT-M3-23**, Figure 30.11, gives **Va pru/rā? ha?**, with a
proposed Varaha interpretation. Both the middle alternative and final uncertainty
are visible in printed page 365. It compares letter styles with Kushana–Satavahana
writing of the first–third centuries CE. An exact 100 CE date is not supplied;
that year falls inside the comparison range, so unsupported precision is the
issue, rather than a chronological contradiction.

Figure 30.11 is a plausible visual match. However, the candidate gives neither
the foil outline nor a field/figure number, and reduces the inscription to three
simple forms. Parent and independent critic examined the cited plate, higher
resolution render and unchanged-coordinate crops. The drawing does not securely
exclude Figure 30.12, another three-syllable foil on that same plate. **Identity
remains provisional.** This adjacent image was used solely to test uniqueness of
the fixed target's join, not to select another inscription matching the reading.
The shared Varaha interpretation cannot itself identify the object.

## Adjudication

If Figure 30.11 is the intended foil, the candidate agrees with an earlier
tentative published interpretation. It is not an independently withheld
prediction: the candidate cites the source containing that interpretation.
Agreement does not establish copying, and this assessment makes no such claim.
The uncertain prior reading also must not become a certain three-syllable key.

The source handles these characters as Brahmi and supplies no identification
of unknown Indus signs with independently known sounds. A first-millennium CE
foil cannot establish continuity from Bronze Age Indus writing simply through
letter resemblance. No sound correspondence, aligned bilingual content or
artifact-level intermediate sequence is established here. A complete reading
of an ordinary Brahmi inscription would still need that additional bridge.

The finite gate is closed: provisional object identity, conditional agreement
with an earlier uncertain interpretation, no qualifying phonetic bridge. Full
candidate reading and mixed-Indus classification are not independently validated;
no binary full-reading falsification or accuracy score is justified. No fitting,
dictionary run, corpus amendment or prior experiment rerun occurred.
Reopen only for an explicit candidate-to-object locator or new independent
reading/sign-continuity evidence, not more image variants or unrestricted foil
screening. Raw material is registered; derived locators, observations, criticism
and pins are in `results/vietnam_gold_foil_cited_reading_gate_v1/`.

The overall goal remains active with no established Indus phonetic/semantic key.
