# Roof-X original photograph and identity review

Date: 2026-09-27. Bounded follow-up to `ROOF_M176_PRIMARY_REVIEW.md`. No meanings or phonetic values assigned.

**EH407 is authenticated as excavation 1722 and its photograph supports the roof-X / comb face and U / two-stroke reverse. The physical relationship between Mahadevan references 4407 and 5444 remains unresolved.** Their identical normalized inscriptions are not evidence of either duplicate photography or independent artifacts.

## Primary identity chain

The already inspected Mahadevan Appendix V rule (printed821/PDF830) explicitly maps references4001–4713 to EH volumeII items1–713. Thus4407→EH407 does not rely on matching digits or text. Vats volumeI printed167 (Archive leaf193), item3 at the bottom, explicitly identifies plateXCV407 as field1722, a tiny shield-shaped steatite seal, squareR24/7, depth15ft6in below surface, length3/4in and width5/16in. The excavation context is the deeper portion east of the trench in MoundAB, with finds below StratumV; Vats cautiously attributes them to StratumVI and earlier. No more precise date is inferred.

Mahadevan printed824/PDF833 explicitly lists5444 as excavation3027(2), photographPu.35:3863. His explanatory paragraph(vi), printed817/PDF826, states that numeral suffixes distinguish multiple objects bearing the same excavation number. The suffix is therefore an object disambiguator, not a face number. Paragraph(iv) states the general practice of collapsing duplicate illustrations of one object to one reference, but a catalogue convention does not independently establish that two particular reference numbers are two physical artifacts.

Vats printed108 (Archive leaf134), item4, identifies plateXCVII558 as field3027, a fragment of a tiny shield-shaped burnt-steatite seal, squareM11/21, depth12ft below surface. No parenthetical suffix is present. This is a real exact field-to-plate bridge, but **not a verified3027(2)→558 bridge**. It is retained as a candidate locator only, not a substitute photograph for5444.

## Independent photograph observations

The original Vats plateXCV, item407, is retained in `vats_1940_plates_9842.pdf`, PDF198. Its two stacked views belong to one numbered object. The upper view shows a left vertical spine with several short horizontal strokes and, on the right, a peaked angular mark above oblique crossed strokes. The angular mark is inside the right lobe, distinct from the physical central notch. This supports the broad roof-over-X morphology of the M77M138 drawing, though the small, coarse scan does not establish precise stroke junctions, spacing, or independent roof-component identity. It is not a fresh pixel-level transcription of every stroke.

The lower view shows a U-like sign at left and two long approximately parallel upright strokes at right. Surface streaking near the central constriction must not be promoted to extra signs. The broad reverse composition agrees with the normalized M77M087/M328 field. Vats calls the reverse one pictogram, whereas M77 represents it with two sign codes; that is a difference in catalogue grouping, not a decoded numerical or semantic value.

Both407 views preserve the paired lobes and opposed central concavities. Vats printed327 (Archive leaf353), Class16/typex, explicitly groups407,556,564 as entire and557,558 as fragmentary. It explains this shield outline as elliptical tablets cut with central semicircular segments along the longer sides. Therefore407's notches are intentional shape, not evidence of a broken tablet. The photograph is coarse and mottled; minor edge wear or fine surface loss cannot be quantified. There is no need to infer a large missing portion of407.

Vats plateXCVII, item558 (PDF202), has two small views of an irregular fragment. Surviving parallel/oblique marks are visible, but this scan does not allow a secure roof-X plus complete reverse reading. Its reduced outline and the explicit fragment description support damage. It cannot be used to authenticate5444's morphology without the missing3027(2) bridge.

## Identity disposition

4407 and5444 have different published source paths and different recorded excavation identifiers (1722 versus3027(2)). This is evidence that the catalogue treats them as separate entries, and is compatible with distinct objects. It does not rule out historical renumbering, a duplicated photograph, or a documentation error. No exact inventory bridge or individually distinctive photograph ofPu.35:3863 was recovered. Physical independence is consequently **not established**, and duplication is also **not established**. Neither record is merged or deleted. Count at most one directly authenticated photographed artifact from this two-reference target set in analyses requiring verified physical independence.
