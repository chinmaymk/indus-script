# Original excavation records for the Chanhu-daro axe and ingot

Mackay's original catalogue authenticates **CH2596A**, the broken axe drawn on Plate LXXIV1/1a, and **CH2529H**, the ingot photographed as LXXIV2. Their dimensions and find contexts are recorded, but no numerical mass for either target appears in the reviewed descriptions or catalogue. The parallel CISI audit identifies these objects as C40 and C39 respectively; that separate catalogue join should accompany any use of those IDs.

| Original record | Dimensions, inches | Find context |
|---|---|---|
| CH2596A, portion of axe, inscriptions on both sides; LXXIV1/1a | 3.43 long × 2.67 wide × 0.28 thick | Square 8/C, locus 297, +9.3 ft above datum; group 2596, in a large pottery pan |
| CH2529H, ingot; LXXIV2 | 13.75 long × average 1.4 diameter | Square 8/C, locus 297, +10.4 ft above datum; group 2529 on courtyard floor |

The exact records are on printed p.305, Archive leaf 325. Page178 explicitly identifies the axe representations as line drawings. The plate mixes those drawings with photographs of other objects; its ingot photograph cannot be treated as a sign tracing. Material descriptions are also limited: the blade-axe group appears to Mackay to be hard bronze from its colour, and he likewise identifies the ingot as bronze from colour on p.187. These are not new chemical analyses.

The catalogue heading on p.305 lists groups 2529 and 2593, but the first item row explicitly reads 2596A. This header omits the axe's group; it does not justify changing its field number. Items 10–17 below belong to 2593, so the header has a legitimate connection to other entries on the plate. We retain the incomplete header separately and use the exact axe row, corroborated by pan 2596 on p.43, for the object join. The cause of the omission is unestablished.

## Layout and earlier interpretation

The axe drawing has five short upright marks above a vertical column of three downward-opening arches. The column crosses the height occupied by the surrounding sign row. A human-shaped sign is immediately beside it. Mackay himself considered whether an adjacent mark was part of a staff held by the human or an additional stroke. His proposed reading of 35 or 36 on p.178 was conditional, as was his suggestion that the marks counted an owner's implements rather than weighing the object. Neither interpretation is adopted here as a demonstrated reading.

![Original drawn signs on the broken axe](../results/primary_pages/c40-original-axe-signs.png)

Thus, the segmentation caveat predates Pettersson's discussion. Repetition of the same interpretation in later work is not independent evidence. The photograph of the ingot on LXXIV2 shows a long irregular bar but does not permit a secure independent recount of its signs in this scan. No `XXUUU` reading is newly authenticated from that original photograph.

## Context separates the groups

Page 43 places pan 2596 in the northeastern corner of courtyard 297, below the adjacent door-jamb. The material numbered 2529 was spread over the courtyard floor, at an average +10.4 ft. Page 175 reports 37 pieces in group 2529 and says salt had cemented its contents together; some objects were too poorly preserved to illustrate. The 37-piece count belongs to the ingot's group, not to the axe's pan, and cannot validate the hypothetical 35/36 axe reading.

There is an unresolved source detail: p.43 includes an ingot among pan 2596's contents, whereas p.305 explicitly assigns the illustrated long ingot to 2529H. Pages 187–188 describe only two ingots, the second unillustrated and numbered 1113 from another trench. The reviewed report therefore does not justify silently placing C39 inside the C40 pan. An unidentified ingot or inconsistency in the report remains possible. Sharing a courtyard does not establish the same owner, deposition event, or meaning.

Page 187 interprets the long ingot as cast in a vertical hole in the ground. That manufacturing account is attributed to the excavator; no new surface analysis verifies it. The numerical weight on the same page belongs to a shovel, LXXIV13, and must not be transferred to the ingot. No mass reconstruction is made from the reported dimensions.
