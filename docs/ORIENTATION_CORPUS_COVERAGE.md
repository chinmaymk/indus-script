# Two stroke-only tool fields are included in M77

The tentative omission claim does not survive correction of the plate locators.
Mahadevan's Appendix V, printed page 818 / PDF page 827, explicitly maps
**2926 to FEM CXXVII item 1** and **2928 to FEM CXXXIII item 1**. The earlier
search for these objects among CXXVI items 1 and 4 used incorrect locators.
No claim of intentional exclusion can be based on that search.

| M77 reference | FEM plate/item | Printed text metadata | Normalized reading-order tokens | Original direction code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2926 | CXXVII.1 = DK7857 | 600004 | M104 M110 | 4: top to bottom |
| 2928 | CXXXIII.1 = DK7800 | 600005 | M104 M104 | 5: symmetrical arrangement of signs |

Both rows are visibly present on printed page 85 / PDF page 96. Neither row
has a doubt asterisk or a hatched loss span. Their metadata identifies bronze
implements, an only side, no field symbol, and an only line. The normalized
M104 is a four-stroke group and M110 a seven-stroke group, checked against the
numbered sign list on printed page 32 / PDF page 43. This authenticates the
catalogue's graphic grouping, not the signs' numerical values or the original
artifacts' completeness. No side or line has been recovered from an omission in
this step: both strings were already in the secondary candidate export.

The original excavation catalogue tables complete the joins: printed page 489
identifies CXXVII.1 as DK7857, and printed page 493 identifies CXXXIII.1 as
DK7800. Both exact table crops were independently re-viewed for this coverage
audit after the original-tool investigation found them. This result is secure
through plate/item as well as the printed excavation number. In particular, a text-search
hit for `7800` in an excavation volume cannot itself identify the knife: an
unrelated seal-table occurrence also exists. Direction code 5 is explicitly
**symmetrical arrangement**, not uncertainty; code 9 is the catalogue's doubtful
direction category. The present normalized right-to-left display is not evidence
of the ancient order of the orthogonal groups.

## What the catalogue says about inclusion

Three primary passages were rendered and inspected:

- **Section 3.6, printed 5 / PDF 16:** Mahadevan generally required direct objects,
  casts or photographs. He excluded secondary copies when no original source was
  available, but allowed a partial exception for drawings in the original
  excavation reports, especially copper tablets, bronze implements and small
  sealings. He says most of those were additionally checked from objects or
  unpublished photographs. This general statement does not prove which source
  was inspected for either individual tool.
- **Section 5.1, printed 6 / PDF 17:** of 3,455 catalogue objects, 549 were excluded
  because inscriptions were absent or totally missing, damaged or illegible;
  2,906 objects with wholly or partly legible inscriptions formed the text corpus.
- **Sections 11.3–11.4, printed 15 / PDF 26:** isolated figures were treated as
  script signs if attested in sign sequences or recognizable as modifications of
  attested signs. The signary normalizes variants, including some rotated through
  angles up to 90 degrees. The author keeps doubtful variant identifications
  separate. This is a classification method, not a proven equivalence of meanings.

These passages do not state a blanket exclusion of simple stroke-only fields.
The two explicit rows above demonstrate inclusion of these particular fields.
Conversely, no conclusion about all excavated simple marks follows from two
included objects. Establishing population coverage would require a complete
object inventory and individual source comparison.
