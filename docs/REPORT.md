## 1. Sign order contains reproducible information

Distinct texts average 4.59 signs in this selected corpus. Their neighboring-sign
mutual information is **3.196 bits**, compared with a mean **2.114 bits** after
shuffling signs across texts while preserving every exact length-and-position
frequency. None of 499 null draws reaches the observed value: the finite permutation
p-value is **0.002**. This rejects that specific independent-position null.

The endpoint distributions also differ strongly: Jensen–Shannon divergence is
**0.703 bits**, with p = 0.002 under within-text shuffling. Order matters beyond the
bag of signs in each text. The same qualitative results occur in the small independent
transcription and the published bar-seal subset. Those corpora overlap archaeologically
and are supporting checks, not independent discoveries.

The neighboring-sign result survives restrictions to good/fine objects, complete
preservation, Harappa alone, Mohenjo-daro alone, and texts without the selected tally
families. All five sensitivity checks reach their 199-draw floor, p = 0.005.

These are new calculations in the saved pipeline, consistent with earlier
[n-gram research](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0009506).
The datasets differ, so this is not an exact reproduction of that paper.

Evidence: [permutation tests](../results/permutation_tests.json),
[sensitivity results](../results/hypothesis_checks.json).

## 2. Local dependencies predict held-out inscriptions

Five-fold evaluation keeps exact duplicate texts and connected artifact records
together. Hyperparameters are selected using a separate validation fold. The models
are given text length and predict signs, without translations or dictionaries.

| Model | Held-out bits per sign ↓ | Perplexity ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sign frequency only | 7.085 | 135.75 |
| Position only, with smoothing | 6.792 | 110.81 |
| Previous sign | **5.934** | **61.14** |
| Previous sign + position | 6.178 | 72.42 |

The simple bigram improves on sign frequency by **1.151 bits/sign** and on position
by **0.858 bits/sign**. Conditional group-bootstrap 95% intervals are respectively
[1.082, 1.225] and [0.810, 0.903]. The more elaborate model performs worse than the
bigram; the data do not justify preferring it merely because it is more complex.

The combined model also beats the two simpler baselines when testing Harappa against
other sites, and Mohenjo-daro against other sites, after removing exact shared strings
from training. This suggests some conventions generalize across those sites. It does
not establish that every site used the same language.

[every held-out loss and group assignment](../results/prediction_lipi.csv).

## 3. Numerical or quantity-like signs are a useful partial hypothesis

The attributed catalogue distinguishes tall and short series of repeated vertical
strokes. We kept those series separate. There are **24 family-specific substitution
frames** in which signs with different described stroke counts occupy the same
position while all other signs stay unchanged.

One concrete frame is `? W700`, observed with tall-stroke signs W031, W032, W033,
and W034: graphically one, two, three, and four strokes. The candidate M correspondences
are M086, M087, M089, and M095. Another frame, `W154 ?`, has short-stroke alternatives
with three, four, and five strokes.

These are good places to seek quantity readings. **They do not establish a particular
unit, commodity, numeral pronunciation, or numerical meaning for every stroke sign.**
An emblem or phonetic system can also show substitutions. Independent quantitative
context is needed to discriminate them. Similar numerical-function arguments have
been explored in [primary research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-019-0274-1);
we have not assumed its semantic conclusions.

Evidence: [all tally frames](../results/tally_substitution_frames.csv),
[other recurring substitution pairs](../results/substitution_pairs.csv).

The [quantity-anchor audit](QUANTITY_ANCHORS.md) now separates proposed meanings
from independently measured quantities. Exact calculations show why capacity alone
cannot identify numerical scale, why an assumed photographic scale is not a new
calibration, and why a reconstructed count interval can admit multiple numeral
assignments. These results limit the strength of proposed readings; they do not
refute quantity notation.

The [numeral-equation extension](NUMERAL_EQUATIONS.md) solves two published
conditional equations completely. With the proposal's minimum-base screen, both
(L,S,v) = (10,8,6) and (36,20,4) survive. An additional disputed contents anchor
selects the smaller solution. This preserves a compact candidate while making its
dependence on external evidence explicit; it is not an established all-sign key.

## 4. Frequent shapes constrain readings but do not fix them

The catalogue associates the jar-shaped M342 with W740. Under that provisional
correspondence, W740 occurs 900 times across distinct texts: **655 at file start,
243 internally, and 2 at file end**. With the supported left-to-right display
convention, file start is the customary reading end of a right-to-left line.

It is therefore a strong candidate for a recurrent final element or phrase-level
marker, among other possibilities. But a universal rule “M342 only ends a complete
text” has **245 counterinstances** in this corpus. A literal vessel, category sign,
name component, suffix, and phonetic value remain distinct competing readings.
The shape alone cannot choose among them.

Fish-like shapes and their modified forms are also useful families for comparison.
Calling a shape “fish” does not establish that its ancient reading was the word for
fish, a homophone of that word, a star name, or any particular syllable. No rebus key
is adopted here.

Evidence: [W sign profiles](../results/sign_profiles_wells.csv),
[417-sign atlas](../results/sign_atlas.html).

## 5. Why this is not a decipherment

The published weather-icon corpus also rejects both arrangement nulls for neighboring
dependence, at p = 0.002. Its absolute statistics differ, and its sample is not matched
to Indus, so this is not evidence that the two systems are equivalent. It is a concrete
counterexample to treating significant sign structure as sufficient proof of language.
The [author-published comparison datasets](https://richardsproat.com/data/non-linguistic-symbols/)
allow this check to be repeated.

There is also a mathematical identification problem: consistently replacing sign
IDs with arbitrary “word” labels leaves their counts, entropies, and n-gram likelihoods
unchanged. The saved code demonstrates this for all 575 observed identifiers. Thus
even a key covering every sign can fit perfectly without establishing ancient meaning.
See the [proof and its explicit scope](IDENTIFIABILITY.md).

The corpus contains **214 sign types occurring only once** after deduplication.
Uncertain sign splitting, rare forms, missing context, and short texts limit what can
be learned for those signs. Assigning confident meanings to them would add unsupported
assumptions rather than evidence.
