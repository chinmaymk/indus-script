# Original-photo check: CH2428 and CH2529CC

The original published impressions support **certain fish-like morphology on CH2428**, and are **consistent with the fixed-family negative classification of CH2529CC**. The negative is a more limited visual result: no member of the declared family is recognizable in the preserved text band, but this coarse reproduction does not independently authenticate every catalogue variant or every W/M code. Neither result assigns a meaning, pronunciation, commodity or occupation.

This is a source-exposed follow-up to the known positive and negative in [CHANHU_DEPOSIT_RESULTS.md](CHANHU_DEPOSIT_RESULTS.md), not blinded coding or fresh replication. The fixed family remains P050, P051, P056–P064 from the earlier fish protocol. Compounds excluded by that protocol remain excluded; no family boundary was changed after inspection.

## CH2428: presence survives uncertainty elsewhere

In LII19, the upper inscription band visibly contains an upright closed-bodied form with lateral projections and a forked lower extension, between more complex forms to its image-left and a pair of long strokes to its image-right. Its overall construction is recognizably the plain fish-like shape used by the declared family. The reproduction supports presence without requiring a complete reading of the band or of adjacent fine details. It is compatible with the export's plain W220/P050 label; an exact M059/W220 equivalence is not independently established for every stroke by this review.

The left part of the inscription is less clear. I cannot establish every missing or faint element, nor whether the uncertainty comes from surface condition, unfinished work, the impression, lighting or reproduction. The export itself has `complete=N` and an initial000 in its string, despite object preservation being marked complete and condition Good. These fields describe different things and must not be collapsed into a claim that the entire text is known. A securely visible family member still supports positive presence when another part is unknown. No exact sign count or complete retranscription is claimed here.

## CH2529CC: no visible fixed-family member; qualified negative

LII23 preserves an upper band across the visible width. Its image-left portion contains a large enclosing oval/curved structure with internal angular detail and adjoining strokes; other visible forms include open angular and upright structures. The internally detailed enclosing form does not show the simple fish-like body's lateral projections and forked extension seen on LII19. No clear plain or declared modified fish-like member is visible. The band does not show an obvious broken-out strip that would independently force a missing-text classification.

This is compatible with the export's `complete=Y`, preservation complete and the absence of declared-family codes from its five-token string. It is not an independent guarantee that every fine mark has been distinguished, that there are exactly five ancient signs, or that all possible fish-like compounds are absent. In particular, broad visual similarity of an oval outline alone does not justify moving an enclosing compound into the fixed family. The review supports the existing operational negative at the visible-band level, with moderate rather than exhaustive variant-authentication confidence. Higher-quality photographs could still change a fine classification.

## Photograph versus impression and direction

Original printed145 says that LII1–6 and11 were photographed directly because impressions were unsafe; the remainder of the seal-amulets on LI/LII are photographs of plasticine impressions. Items19 and23 consequently belong to that stated impression group. The retained images were neither mirrored nor rotated in this task. An impression and a seal face reverse lateral layout; no reading direction is inferred from the present photographs or imposed from the export's R/L label. “Image-left” and “image-right” above describe only the published impression as seen.
