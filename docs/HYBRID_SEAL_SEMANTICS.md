# Hybrid seals: a patronymic hypothesis with no established Indus equivalent

Published accounts describe two seals combining Indus-style bull imagery with cuneiform. Their proposed
readings make personal identification a reasonable comparative hypothesis for
some seal legends. They do not translate any Indus sign. One cuneiform text is
badly worn; the other lacks a recovered accession record, original reading
publication, and a photograph examined in this audit.

## Paris seal: what the source actually establishes

Vidale's2005 paper, *The Short-Horned Bull on the Indus Seals*, was downloaded
from the [DAI edition](https://doi.org/10.34780/mvc3-3u6b). Printed pages151–153
and156 were rendered and visually checked. On p152, the author describes a
seal held by the Cabinet des Médailles in Paris, reportedly bought in Beirut.
The possible Lebanese or Syrian findspot is conjectural. No museum inventory
number, stratified context or object photograph appears in this discussion.
Searches of indexed BnF records did not identify its accession; unrelated
Gudea and administrative tablets were rejected as matches.

The reading is attributed to Glassner2002, p361 note215:
`Ur.dNin.Ildum dumu Ur.gi7`. Vidale describes a personal name or attribute and a
patronymic. His discussion allows competing interpretations of the final element
and cites D'Agostino's personal communication. The carpenter-deity association
leads to suggestions about timber trade and inherited occupations, which the
author himself treats cautiously. It does not identify goods sealed by this
object. A name involving a deity does not establish the owner's occupation.

The earlier Vidale2004 account, printed264–265, was available through public
web extraction and gives the same preliminary source chain. Its direct PDF
download returned403. This corroborates what Vidale reported, rather than
independently confirming Glassner's reading. The original Glassner chapter was
located bibliographically but not recovered. No cuneiform signs were newly
collated against a Paris artifact image, and no Indus transcription was assigned.

The frequently cited *Hybrid Identities* study cannot fill that gap merely by
being mentioned in bibliographies. Frenez's public university CV, dated
10July2024, lists the collaborative paper under work in preparation on PDFp7
and lists2014 presentations separately. The2023 review cites a2014 conference
presentation. This establishes publication status in those documents, not a
claim that no later publication exists. This search recovered no final article
with a new photographic collation or secure Paris accession.

## Ur seal: why the reading must retain alternatives

The independent [BM120573 audit](BM120573_CUNEIFORM_AUDIT.md) examines Gadd's
original prose and plate. The damaged signs admit alternative values; a personal
name remains an interpretation. A common online transcription should not replace
the values actually printed in the primary sources. Neither the bull motif nor
the seal's shape constitutes a second, translated text. Stylistic similarity
supports a comparison of use, but not equality of inscription content.

## A testable proposal and its limits

Vidale's p156 proposes that human-shaped or paired-human signs in western Indus
inscriptions could mark descent. He suggests looking for linked expressions
such as `A D B` and `B D C`, where `D` is a proposed relation marker. His stronger
claim that domestic seals lack patronymics is not established by the absence of
a then-recognized pattern. Fixed position, recurrence and short text length do
not themselves distinguish names from offices, commodities or other categories.

The [computational investigation](PATRONYMIC_TEST_DESIGN.md) tests the specific
internal-marker construction against the available corpus, preserves the
unresolved mapping of paired-human variants, and considers other sign delimiters.
A failed internal-marker construction would not exclude prefix constructions,
multisign relation markers, variant spellings, or naming systems without
patronymics. A matched chain would still require independent names, chronology
and artifact identities before it could be read as genealogy.

The defensible contribution is therefore a constrained hypothesis and a source
correction: some seal legends may identify persons or affiliations, while the
current comparative evidence does not choose meanings for Indus characters.
No individual semantic or phonetic value is established by these seals.

## Reproduction

All downloaded source bytes remain in `data/raw/`. The
[source audit script](../scripts/audit_hybrid_sources.py) re-extracts the two
downloaded PDFs, verifies the saved evidence fingerprints, and records the
publication-status and reading limitations in machine-readable form. Page images
are derived files in `results/primary_pages/`. Neither source prose nor OCR
is silently promoted to an authenticated artifact reading.
