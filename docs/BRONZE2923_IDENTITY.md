# M77 reference 2923: identity and limits of the mass evidence

**Reference 2923 is the already studied broken chisel/tang, FEM Plate CXXVI item 2, conventionally DK7856.** It is not an additional tool with a new independent mass measurement. The exact join is printed in Mahadevan's Appendix V, p.818 (PDF827). The adjacent rows separately identify 2924 as CXXVI3 and 2925 as CXXVI5.

| M77 reference | Primary plate and item | Conventional identity | Reported surviving mass |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 2923 | FEM CXXVI2 | DK7856, broken chisel/tang | 165.343 g |
| 2924 | FEM CXXVI3 | DK7855, small axe | 262.233 g |
| 2925 | FEM CXXVI5 | Primary DK7535; later DK7835 | 1910.030 g |

The [exact appendix crop](../results/primary_pages/bronze2923-appendix-detail.png) supports the crosswalk. The plate/item identifiers join to Mackay's mass discussion on printed p.454 and catalogue table on p.489. The chisel's field number is blurred in the retained original scan; the third digit can resemble 6. That digit uncertainty does not affect the unambiguous plate/item join. The separate 7535/7835 discrepancy concerns 2925, not 2923.

Mackay's catalogue records CXXVI2 as bronze, 5.22 inches long, 0.91 inches wide, with a tang thickness of 0.30 inches. It gives block 12A, house I, room 15, level −24.4 feet. The original plate heading supplies Mohenjo-daro, DK area, G section. Dimensions describe the surviving fragment. Page473 classifies it as a chisel with a broad flattened tang narrowing toward its edge, and estimates an original length above 15 inches. That estimate is not an intact measurement or a mass multiplier.

The newly recovered M77 side2 transcription is `M387 M015 M104 M106 M312`, with original direction code4 retained. On the original CXXVI2 drawing, the corresponding face has two complex signs above four transverse strokes, five upright strokes and one arch. The other face has a different inscription. This supplies an exact object-level connection between the recovered stroke occurrence and the previously audited mass hypothesis. The source is a published drawing, with the signs placed in light patches on a dark silhouette; it is not a new reading of the physical incisions.

The [plate audit](METAL_PLATE_AUDIT.md) tentatively associates photograph CXXXI31 with this tang. The original chisel discussion on p.473 includes CXXXI31 in the same broad type list, but does not explicitly pair it with CXXVI2. Neither this passage nor visual similarity upgrades that photograph to a secure individual join. No CISI number or current museum accession has been authenticated in this bounded review. Searching the retained, incomplete CISI1 OCR and three targeted web queries produced no new primary museum record; this is not evidence that no such record exists.

The mass does not resolve the stroke values. Mackay reports that the implements were corroded together and separated in a chemical bath, considerably reducing their weights. He identifies item2 as a broken tang and considers its marks older than the break. The complete ancient mass, the removed portion, and the corrosion loss remain unknown. The coexistence of four transverse and five upright strokes neither proves equal value per stroke nor establishes that their expression denotes mass. The decimal places in the reported gram value also do not establish milligram weighing accuracy.

The independent mass-anchor count therefore does **not** increase. Treating 2923 and DK7856 as separate observations would count the same damaged artifact twice. This result redirects the numerical inquiry toward an independently identified intact object or a reliable conservation record, rather than another fit to the known fragment.
