# Independent method review: fish signs and the Harappa 8650 assemblage

25 September 2026. This review assesses identification and test design; it does
not supply a sign reading. No new data were downloaded for this review.

## Evidence inspected and its limits

I read the current project report, the retained Vats 1940 OCR around printed
pp. 58–60, and the text layer of Mukhopadhyay's *Bulletin of the IRC* No. 6 (2025),
printed pp. 3, 10–11 (PDF pp. 9, 16–17). The latter is the 50-page bulletin file
`data/raw/irc_2025_fish_bulletin.pdf`, not the separate proceedings version.
I also visually checked the retained original scans of Vats pp. 60, 348, 349 and
435, plus Plate XCVI. The
parent investigation is checking the remaining page images and inscription
identities. This review does not treat OCR labels or the six-type count as
independently authenticated transcriptions.

The bulletin reports six distinct inscriptions in series 8650, four with fish
signs, counting identical inscriptions once; it adds three nearby fish tablets.
Vats associates the group with squares H12/21 and I12/1 at depths 4–7 ft. His list
includes a group of nine tablets and another four tablets, alongside diverse beads,
other ornaments, two weights and other objects. On p. 60 he also mentions two
nearby tablets 10928a and eight later seals/tablets from the same area. Therefore
the three nearby fish tablets cannot be treated as three successes in three
trials: the full eligible nearby inventory and each text's preservation must be
reconstructed first. The original p. 60 places 10928a in square I11/15; its OCR's
I11/13 is incorrect.

**Correction after primary-table and plate review:** an initial conditional
object-weighted calculation of **7/17** assumed that Figure 4's single panel for
8650b represented nine identical non-fish objects. That assumption is not
supported by the original catalogue and must not be used as an observed count.
The arithmetic remains valid only under the rejected provisional classification;
it is not a correction to the author's four-of-six claim.

Plate XCVI illustrates those numbered items with visibly different complete

U-shaped form. Item 489's lower face has a different sequence of angular and

a compact geometric cluster above. These are gross visual contrasts, not fine
sign-code assignments. Together with the find-number links they contradict
collapsing the entire 8650b group into the single full inscription pictured for
433. Vats's narrative phrase about identical pictograms cannot safely override
the catalogue and plates; its precise intended scope remains unresolved.

Seven illustrated catalogue rows do not automatically establish seven physical
objects or seven unique inscription types, and this review does not supply a
corrected fish numerator, denominator or full transcription. The nine-object
total, duplicated exemplars and table-to-object multiplicities need complete
reconciliation. The broken 8650g remains unclassified. The parent investigation
also found a page-map error: actual printed p. 348 is leaf376, not leaf374
(which is printed p. 346). The visual printed page, rather than the erroneous
map, grounds the links above.

The excavation report describes painstaking collection over three months using
knives and sieves. That supports careful recovery of small finds; it does not
by itself make the two squares and three-foot depth interval a sealed deposit,
one occupational event, one workshop or one owner's collection. The phrase
about a well and fourteen workmen's quarters describes the stratum. Its precise
spatial relationship to the 8650 finds needs the plan and detailed excavation
account, rather than inheriting the entire stratum's interpretation. There is
also an unresolved primary-source discrepancy: p. 435 assigns the 8650q beads in
the same two squares and depth interval to **Stratum III**, whereas the earlier
narrative is under **Stratum IV**. Do not silently resolve this as a typo or
claim it proves mixed deposition; preserve the discrepancy pending further
records.

## Which proposition could the association test?

These are different propositions, each requiring additional evidence:

1. Fish-bearing text types are more frequent in an independently classified
   jewellery-related archaeological context than in comparable other contexts.
2. The difference reflects the context of the original inscription use, rather
   than manufacture, storage, secondary deposition or differential recovery.
3. A fish sign denotes a commodity or craft, rather than a person, institution,
   status, quantity, ritual category or another correlate of that context.
4. Its semantic range is gemstones or precious shiny commodities specifically.
5. It had a particular Dravidian pronunciation, rebus mechanism or word value.

Even a successful test of 1 does not establish 2–5. An institutional mark used by
a lapidary organization and a gemstone classifier can generate the same observed
fish–workshop association. Context alone leaves those models observationally
indistinguishable. The bulletin's prior interpretation of tablets as trade/craft
licenses must remain a hypothesis, not an independently measured covariate that
establishes the fish meaning. Nor do nearby uninscribed weights attach a measured
quantity to any particular inscription.

## Required counting and comparison rules

- **Inventory first.** Crosswalk every included object to excavation number,
  plate, corpus identifier, face and full text. Record missing or uncertain
  fields explicitly. Distinguish physical objects, faces, inscription lines,
  complete multi-face inscription types and duplicated mould/text types.
- **Fix the fish family.** Specify which unmodified, modified, compound and
  damaged forms qualify before counting. Keep uncertainty bounds and report
  results both for an unambiguous core and for an explicitly expanded family.
  Shape similarity alone does not establish one semantic family.
- **Use one unit per comparison.** A baseline of inscription lines cannot be
  substituted for a baseline of distinct multi-face text types. The bulletin's
  approximate 1250/5500 line frequency is neither a verified denominator here nor
  a directly comparable reference for its four-of-six deduplicated types.
- **Respect clustering.** Duplicate objects, where authenticated, do not supply
  independent semantic replications. Conversely, deduplicating the target but leaving
  duplicates in controls changes the estimand. Show object-weighted and
  type-weighted counts separately and model dependence at deposit/workshop
  level. With only one proposed workshop, there is no independent estimate of
  between-workshop variability.
- **Match controls without reading their signs.** Use the same site, period,
  object class, material, preservation and text-length distribution as far as
  the records allow. A longer or multi-face text has more opportunity to contain
  a fish. Tablet/seal differences and differing recovery practices can also
  create an apparent craft association.
- **Keep the boundary fixed.** Report 8650 separately from later same-area finds,
  the 10928a pair and wider nearby finds. Define spatial/depth inclusion before
  inspecting outcomes; display every eligible excluded and damaged object.
  Boundary sensitivity is evidence about robustness, not permission to choose
  the radius giving the strongest result.

## Quantitative sensitivity: an illustration, not an empirical test

If six independent, exchangeable types had each had a *known* matched baseline
fish probability p, a fixed, prospectively chosen four-or-more criterion would
give

`P(X >= 4 | n=6,p) = 15*p^4 - 24*p^5 + 10*p^6`.

| Hypothetical matched p | Probability of at least 4 of 6 |
|---|---:|
|0.20|0.01696|
|0.30|0.07047|
|0.40|0.17920|
|0.50|0.34375|

The one-sided 0.05 crossing is p≈0.27133837. This sensitivity shows why the absent
matched denominator matters. These numbers are **not** p-values for the actual
assemblage: independence, exchangeability, baseline estimation and prospective
selection have not been established. Trying many sign families, sites, materials
or context boundaries would further invalidate treating the selected example as
one predetermined test. Four of six alone supplies no enrichment estimate: the
comparison rate is unknown. This calculation used Python's standard-library
binomial coefficients and agrees with the polynomial above.

## Concrete falsifiable next tests

1. **Reconstruct the claimed numerator and denominator.** Authenticate all six
   full inscription types on primary illustrations, including the two non-fish
   types. Enumerate every same-area and nearby inscribed object, including
   incomplete ones. A failure to recover the six-type count revises that count;
   it does not by itself refute a general gemstone hypothesis.
2. **Build an independent context table.** Archaeological reviewers should label
   complete eligible deposits as production, storage, consumption, mixed or
   unknown using waste, tools, unfinished objects, architecture and stratigraphy
   without inspecting the inscriptions. Beads alone need not identify a
   workshop. Preserve evidence for the label and label uncertainty.
3. **Test association in unselected deposits.** Fix the fish set, context labels,
   sampling unit and contrast in a protocol, then evaluate contexts not used to
   develop the present theory. Compare matched fish presence or prevalence;
   report effect sizes and uncertainty, with deposit-level dependence. Contexts
   already discussed by the author are discovery data even if new to this
   project. Sparse/no-overlap strata are inconclusive, not zero effects.
4. **Demand specificity.** Compare gemstone production with other craft,
   administrative, residential and ritual contexts, and compare fish with other
   equally frequent sign families. A generic excess of many signs in rich,
   long-text administrative finds supports no uniquely gemstone interpretation.
   Separate stone beads, precious metal and faience predictions rather than
   broadening “shiny commodity” after encountering mismatches.
5. **Seek a meaning-discriminating anchor.** Independently identified contents
   in an original sealed package, an authenticated bilingual correspondence, or
   repeated controlled associations with distinct commodities could discriminate
   a commodity term from institution/person/quantity alternatives. Each must
   link to the same artifact and inscription, rather than merely a shared area.
   Phonetic claims require separate evidence even after such semantic progress.

A universal “every fish inscription labels gemstones” claim would face a genuine
counterexample only when an independently known original referent contradicts it
under a fixed semantic scope. A fish seal excavated outside a lapidary workshop
is not sufficient: seals and goods move. Conversely, unrestricted metaphor,
rebus substitution and arbitrary scope expansion can rescue any association and
therefore cannot count as a falsifiable decipherment.

The present assemblage is a useful archaeological lead, with several concrete
ways to test it. It currently supplies neither an independently demonstrated
fish meaning nor values across the 417-sign inventory. All semantic and
phonetic fields should remain unknown pending stronger anchors.
