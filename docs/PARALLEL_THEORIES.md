# Parallel tests of three explicit theories

Three separate investigations tested different assumptions using the pinned data.
They ran concurrently after the user's authorization. They are not three independent
corpora or archaeological confirmations: all share source limitations. The parent
reviewed the scripts, denominators, tests, and interpretation before integration.
No additional data were downloaded in this phase.

| Explicit hypothesis tested | New evidence | Supported conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| A copper-associated singleton or entire text always identifies the object's pictured motif | W777 occurs on H-598 with a different source motif label; a rhinoceros-linked whole text occurs on M-1803 with a different label | The universal caption rule encounters source-level counterexamples; generic animal words and copper-specific conventions remain possible |
| Every contiguous tally-like run is one numeral with one global graphic-class order | Best of six orders fails 26 of 186 distinct-text runs; Good M-160 and M-678 contain the same codes in opposite orders | This universal ordered-field assumption fails on the literal corpus; additive or multiple-field alternatives remain possible |
| Additional preceding signs improve prediction beyond shorter context | At specified smoothing, two-sign context gains 0.00734 bits over a sharpening control; first-sign residual is 0.000237 with interval spanning zero | Some short combinations help under these model choices; additional first-sign memory is inconclusive |

The tests do not assign ancient words or sounds. Rejecting a strict assumption is
not equivalent to selecting a replacement decipherment. Nor do modest predictive
gains identify linguistic writing: weather-icon controls also benefit from longer
context.

## Where the evidence is strongest and weakest

The [primary numeral follow-up](NUMERAL_PRIMARY_FOLLOWUP.md) and
[H-598 photograph check](ICONOGRAPHY_PHOTO_FOLLOWUP.md) strengthen particular
observations after the initial parallel tests. They retain explicit limits on
object crosswalks, exact W variants and numerical-field segmentation.

The numeral result has an exact logical core: two ordered graphic classes cannot
both precede one another under one fixed rank order. Reversing every text together
does not remove that conflict. Applying the proof to these artifacts remains
conditional on correct source transcription and the assumption that each adjacent
run is a single field. No physical quantity has thereby been established.

The imagery result survives a Good/Fair source filter for W777/H-598. A later
photograph check confirms its compound glyph and single-horn animal on the same
impression face, while the exact W-code variant and copper counterpart remain
unverified. An excavation-number match also locates an earlier drawing. The
whole-text M-1803 transfer depends on a Poor copper discovery witness. Only two
external objects are testable; most candidates have no external observations.

The structural result is small and sensitive to smoothing. Its calibration control
was added after initial results and retains child-context support. Site/type
partitioning reduces the remaining two-sign advantage further. These are predictive
comparisons with artifact/duplicate grouping, not formal tests of grammatical or
semantic structure. No result here establishes word boundaries.
