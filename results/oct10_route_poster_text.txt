Since 2023, the MAFBI programme focuses on Mound I, little-investigated in 
the 1930s, with the objective to document the complete occupation sequence of 
Chanhu-daro covering the entire urban period (2600/2500-1900 BCE) and the 
post-Indus period (2nd millennium BCE). The results of excavations in Trenches 
4, 5, 6 and 7 showed that a large part of Mound I was formed by mudbrick 
architectural remains associated with 1stIndus period ceramics (2600/2500-2300 
BCE). They are topped with fired brick architectural deposits dated from the 2nd
and 3rdIndus periods (2300-1900 BCE). Jhukar and post-Indus levels were also 
unearthed at the top and bottom of the mound. No evidence of alluvial 
sediments deposited by major floods was observed in the long Trench 7 cut 
between Mound I and Mound II. The preliminary results of a deep core-drilling 
indicate that the archaeological deposits continue at least 10 to 15 meters below 
the current ground level.
The mudbrick buildings unearthed in Trenches 4, 5, 6 and 
7, comparable to those excavated in Mound II include 
small rooms and courtyards with kitchen areas and large 
storage jars installed into mudbrick compartments. There 
were associated with significant quantities of pottery and 
various objects. Among the most remarkable discoveries 
was a hidden small pot, found in Trench 6, that contained a 
complete necklace composed of 119 beads in carnelian, 
steatite, lapis lazuli, amazonite and shell, and an incised 
steatite button. This necklace was embedded in a cluster of 
12-15,000 steatite microbeads.
A specialized steatite workshop was uncovered in Trench 6. It provided with 7151 chips and
1037 preforms and beads of various types. The building includes different rooms for cutting the 
raw material, pre-forming, shaping and firing the beads. Several of them provided very 
numerous fragments of plaques and cylinders in fired talc belonging to heating stands or kinds 
of refractory kiln racks.

Ar the top of the mound (Trench 4), the fired brick settlement ascribed to the 2nd Indus period 
(2300-2100 BCE) was built directly on the mudbrick ruins from earlier occupations, with no 
evidence of huge debris layers or levelling work. It includes buildings with courtyards, large 
quadrangular rooms and smaller rooms, hearths, compartments, portions or drains, bathing 
pavements and cesspits. Room floors provided large quantities of ceramic vessels and terracotta 
objects, numerous stone beads, few seals and cubic weights. In Trench 7 dug into the north-
eastern slope of Mound I, the transition level between the mudbrick settlement and the fired 
brick settlement is marked by the presence of a huge retaining wall (6 m wide). The builders 
constructed it against the 1stIndus period ruins to stabilise the collapsed house blocks and allow 
new buildings to be erected. This wall built in mudbrick had a revetment in fired brick, which 
must provide protection against rainfall effects. Excavations also provided with occupation 
levels associated with Jhukar style ceramics at the top and base of Mound I. The stratigraphic 
study has demonstrated that the Jhukar culture arrived on the site during the very last centuries 
of the Indus Civilisation and it continued after its decline.
