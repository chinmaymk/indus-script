By GC. J. GADD
Communicated October 19, 1932

'HE very great interest which has, in the last few years,
been attracted to the,excavations of two early sites,

Mohenjo-Daro and Harappa, in the valley of the Indus,
was first aroused by Sir John Marshall’s revelation of his
discoveries in the autumn of 1924. Notable similarities to
various Mesopotamian antiquities were not long in being
observed and pointed out, but the most remarkable of all
the objects found, the seals engraved with animal-figures
and inscriptions in a completely unknown character, were
not in themselves comparable with Mesopotamian seals,
even if the new signs were, occasionally reminiscent of the
early Sumerian pictographs. It is not a little surprising,
therefore, that in the preceding autumn (1923) a specimen
of these Indian seals had been found by a scientific expedi-
tion! working at the site of the ancient city of Kish, near the
Euphrates. With this evidence alone it became manifest
that the cultural similarities between the two regions must
have depended upon a definite contact in historical times;
at what period could not be inferred from the position in
which the seal was found at Kish, beyond the assurance that
it was at least before 2000 8.c, Early in 1924 a seal of the
